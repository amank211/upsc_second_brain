- Chapter 3 "A Tough Neighbourhood" (pp. 83–120) — India's SAARC-region relations (Afghanistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Myanmar) plus SAARC itself: the structural "big neighbour" asymmetry problem, country-by-country bilateral surveys, and a closing call for regional integration over bilateral favour-giving.
- Chapter 4 "China and India: Competition, Cooperation or Conflict?" (pp. 121–158) — comparative economic/hard-power trajectories, bilateral trade, the unresolved LAC border dispute, Tibet/Dalai Lama, Taiwan, the wider strategic contest (String of Pearls, UNSC), mutual perception surveys, and a closing "room enough for both" framing. Notably, this chapter's 2012 assessment that "fears of imminent hostilities are clearly overblown" was overturned by the 2020 Galwan clash — a strong built-in "how has this aged" analytical hook flagged in its Beyond the Book boxes.
- Chapter 5 "India's 'Near Abroad'" (pp. 159–208) — the North-East as India's "doorway" to Southeast Asia, Look East/BIMSTEC connectivity projects, Central Asia (TAPI, Ayni airbase, SCO), Australia, a Timor-Leste case study in neglected follow-through, and IOR-ARC/the Indian Ocean's strategic salience. Note: corrected the chapter's title mid-session — it is "India's 'Near Abroad'" only, not "...The Arab World and the Rest of Asia" as first (incorrectly) stated; no Arab-world content actually appears in this chapter.
- Chapter 6 "Red, White, Blue and Saffron: The United States and India" (pp. 209–240) — the India-US relationship's arc from Cold War estrangement to "strategic partnership," the Indian-American diaspora's economic/political rise, competing US schools of thought (Perkovich vs. Tellis), trade, the MMRCA/Rafale defence-procurement friction, and the Pakistan/China/Iran-Chabahar limits on cooperation.
