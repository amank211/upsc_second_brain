  Doval heading for China to prepare ground for President Xi's visit to India
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → International Relations → India-China bilateral relations; boundary dispute negotiations; Special Representatives Mechanism
- GS Paper II → International Relations → BRICS Summit; India-China normalisation post-Kazan

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
National Security Adviser Ajit Doval is scheduled to visit Beijing on 24–25 August 2026 for the 25th round of talks between the Special Representatives on the India-China boundary question. His counterpart is Chinese Politburo Member and Foreign Minister Wang Yi. The visit is significant because it precedes a likely visit by Chinese President Xi Jinping to India in September for the BRICS Summit, which India is hosting on 12 September. Both sides last met at the WMCC (Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination) on 6 August 2026, where they reviewed the LAC situation and discussed boundary delimitation, border management, and trans-border cooperation. The previous Special Representatives round — held in India in August 2025 — agreed on a framework guided by the 2005 Agreement on Political Parameters. The trajectory reflects the broader normalisation that began at the Kazan BRICS sidelines in October 2024, with both Modi and Xi affirming in August 2025 at the SCO Summit in Tianjin that the two countries are "development partners and not rivals."
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Special Representatives Mechanism (2003):</strong> Established under the India-China Joint Declaration to resolve the boundary dispute at a political level, operating alongside military-level talks (Border Personnel Meetings). The Indian Special Representative is the NSA; the Chinese is the State Councillor/FM. There have been 25 rounds to date.</li>
<li><strong>Agreement on Political Parameters and Guiding Principles (2005):</strong> The foundational document for settling the India-China boundary question, signed during PM Manmohan Singh's Beijing visit. It lays out a three-stage process: framework agreement, then delimitation on maps, then demarcation on the ground. The 2005 agreement is considered Stage 1's conclusion.</li>
<li><strong>Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination (WMCC):</strong> A diplomatic-level channel established in 2012 to handle LAC-related issues, comprising joint secretary-level officials. It operates between Special Representatives rounds and complements military Corps Commander talks.</li>
<li><strong>BRICS Summit 2026:</strong> India holds the BRICS Chair in 2026 and is hosting the summit on 12 September. An Xi visit would be his first to India since 2019. The Kazan BRICS sidelines in October 2024 were the reset moment, followed by the patrolling agreement restoring positions on the LAC that had been lost after June 2020.</li>
<li><strong>Line of Actual Control (LAC):</strong> The de facto boundary between India and China, roughly 3,488 km long, divided into Western (Ladakh), Middle (Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand), and Eastern (Arunachal Pradesh) sectors. It is not a formally demarcated boundary; differing perceptions of its alignment are the root cause of friction.</li>
</ul>
</div>

  U.S. MoU best way out of war, says Iran President Pezeshkian
</h2>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → International Relations → West Asia geopolitics; Iran-US conflict; Strait of Hormuz; impact on India's energy security
- GS Paper III → Energy Security → Oil imports; LNG; sanctions impact on India

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian defended the Memorandum of Understanding his country signed with the United States in mid-June 2026, describing it as the best available path out of a war that began on 28 February when the U.S. and Israel struck Iran. The 60-day MoU period ended without a breakthrough, particularly on the Strait of Hormuz — which Tehran has brought to a virtual standstill. While Pezeshkian struck a diplomatic tone, the newly appointed head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei, warned neighbouring countries against joining U.S. sanctions efforts, threatening to target alternative Persian Gulf shipping routes if they did. The U.S. Treasury Secretary is expected to announce "the toughest sanctions in history" against Iran. Iran's economy is under severe strain from six months of war, sanctions, and blockade. The conflict has significant implications for India, which imports roughly 80% of its crude oil needs and has seen its crude import bill spike 41% in July partly due to the conflict.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Strait of Hormuz:</strong> A narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of global oil trade passes daily. Iran's threats to close it periodically escalate as a geopolitical pressure lever; closure would trigger global energy price spikes and severely impact India, which imports 85% of its petroleum needs.</li>
<li><strong>Iran's Supreme National Security Council:</strong> The apex body for national security decisions in Iran, chaired by the President but guided by the Supreme Leader. Mohsen Rezaei, a former IRGC commander, was appointed to head it in August 2026, signalling a hardening of Iran's military stance.</li>
<li><strong>India-Iran Energy Relations:</strong> India was one of Iran's largest oil buyers before U.S. sanctions under CAATSA forced it to halt imports in 2019. India also has a strategic interest in the Chabahar Port project (under a 10-year agreement signed in 2024) for trade access to Afghanistan and Central Asia, which has special exemptions from U.S. sanctions.</li>
<li><strong>CAATSA (Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act, 2017):</strong> U.S. legislation that imposes secondary sanctions on entities that transact with Iran, Russia, or North Korea. India has faced CAATSA-related pressure for defence purchases from Russia (S-400) and for oil imports from Iran.</li>
<li><strong>U.S. 100% Tariffs on Russian Oil Importers:</strong> As mentioned in the editorial section, the U.S. is preparing 100% tariffs on countries that import Russian oil, which would further raise costs for Indian exporters. This compounds the energy import burden already elevated by the Iran-U.S. war.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  Search continues for 22 from vessel that sank off Odisha — M.V. Ocean Winner
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#E8EEF6;color:#2858A0;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Defence</span>
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Governance</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 1 · Front Page</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper III → Internal Security → Maritime security; Indian Navy and Coast Guard SAR operations
- GS Paper III → Disaster Management → Search and rescue; maritime coordination

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
The fate of 22 crew members aboard the Panama-flagged bulk carrier M.V. Ocean Winner remained unknown 24 hours after the vessel sank in the Bay of Bengal off Odisha, having departed Paradip Port on 20 August 2026 with a cargo of iron ore. The ship carried 24 crew — 20 Chinese, 3 Myanmarese, 1 Bangladeshi. Two Chinese crew members were rescued from liferafts by the merchant vessel MT Aisopos, which was transiting nearby in coordination with the Indian Navy's P8I maritime surveillance aircraft. The Eastern Naval Command deployed the P8I from Sri Vijaya Puram-based MRCC (Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre). The 28-year-old vessel (IMO 9145530), built in 1998 and 225m long, had a history of deficiencies — the last inspection in June 2026 by Bangladesh noted three issues including a faulty magnetic compass. The vessel's end-of-life status raises questions about port state control enforcement.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC):</strong> India operates MRCCs under the Indian Coast Guard, responsible for coordinating SAR operations in India's Search and Rescue Region (SRR) covering 4.6 million sq km of ocean. The MRCC at Sri Vijaya Puram (Port Blair) covers the Bay of Bengal sector.</li>
<li><strong>P8I Poseidon Maritime Patrol Aircraft:</strong> India operates 12 P-8I aircraft (Boeing), a long-range maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare platform. Fitted with HARPOON missiles and advanced sonar/radar, it is a cornerstone of India's maritime surveillance. India was the first international buyer of the P-8 variant.</li>
<li><strong>Port State Control (PSC):</strong> Under the International Maritime Organization (IMO) framework, port states have the right and duty to inspect foreign vessels in their ports to verify compliance with international conventions (SOLAS, MARPOL, etc.). Repeated deficiency notices, as with Ocean Winner, should ideally trigger detention before the ship sails.</li>
<li><strong>SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea Convention, 1974):</strong> The principal IMO convention for maritime safety, covering structural safety, fire protection, life-saving appliances, and radio communications. Compliance is monitored via port state inspections. A faulty magnetic compass is a SOLAS deficiency.</li>
<li><strong>P&I Insurance (Protection and Indemnity):</strong> A mutual insurance arrangement covering ship-owners against third-party liabilities including crew injury/death, oil pollution, and cargo damage. Ocean Winner's P&I cover was current from August 2026, which means affected crew families have a compensation avenue.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  Congress flags an 'abnormal' 10-day delay in Lok Sabha prorogation — Delimitation Bill angle
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Polity</span>
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Governance</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 1 · Front Page</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → Indian Polity → Parliament; prorogation vs adjournment; Article 85; Delimitation; Constitutional Amendment
- GS Paper II → Indian Polity → Women's Reservation Act; redistribution of seats

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh flagged what he called an "abnormal" 10-day gap between the Lok Sabha's adjournment sine die on 13 August and its prorogation, which had still not occurred as of 24 August 2026. Normal parliamentary practice sees a gap of 2–4 days, though exceptions of 20–28 days have occurred (2015 and 2021 Monsoon Sessions). Ramesh cited the Kaul-Shakdher compendium Practice and Procedure of Parliament to note that prorogation — not adjournment — formally closes a session and allows the next to be convened. He alleged the delay suggests the government is attempting to assemble a two-thirds majority for the Constitution Amendment Bill on Delimitation, which the Modi government introduced in April 2026. The Bill proposes increasing Lok Sabha seats to 850 based on the 2011 Census and implementing 33% reservation for women in tandem. The Opposition defeated the Bill in the Monsoon Session.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Prorogation vs Adjournment (Article 85):</strong> Under Article 85(2), the President prorogates Parliament on the advice of the Prime Minister. Prorogation terminates a session and lapses pending business (except for Bills and select committee reports). Adjournment sine die ends sittings without fixing the next date, but does not end the session — only prorogation does that.</li>
<li><strong>Delimitation:</strong> The process of re-drawing Lok Sabha and State Assembly constituency boundaries based on census data. The Constitution (Article 82) mandates delimitation after each census. It has been frozen since 2001 on the basis of 1971 Census figures to prevent southern states (which have lower population growth) from losing seats. Unfreezing delimitation using the 2011 Census would shift seats toward northern states.</li>
<li><strong>Women's Reservation Act (Constitution 106th Amendment), 2023:</strong> Passed in September 2023, it reserves 33% of Lok Sabha and State Assembly seats for women. However, it contains a trigger clause — it can only be implemented after the next delimitation exercise following the next census. This links women's reservation with the contested delimitation question.</li>
<li><strong>Special Session of Parliament:</strong> Parliament can be convened for a Special Session under Article 85(1) if a session is prorogued and a fresh summons is issued. A Constitutional Amendment requires a two-thirds majority of members present and voting, plus absolute majority of total membership of each House (Article 368).</li>
<li><strong>Monsoon Session 2026:</strong> The Monsoon Session ended on 13 August with the passage of the MMDR Amendment Bill, the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Amendment Bill, and several other Bills, reportedly with limited debate. The Constitution Amendment Bill on Delimitation was defeated.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  Digging for Gold — MMDR Amendment Act 2026 and Odisha's Fiscal Fears
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Federalism</span>
  <span style="background:#E8F2E8;color:#1A5C2A;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Economy</span>
  <span style="background:#E8F2E8;color:#1A5C2A;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Industry</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 2 · States</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → Indian Polity → Federalism; Centre-State relations; Seventh Schedule; State List vs Union List
- GS Paper III → Economy → Mining policy; mineral revenues; MMDR Act; resource federalism

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
The Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act 2026, passed by Parliament on 13 August and receiving Presidential assent, has triggered alarm in mineral-rich Odisha. The Act amends the MMDR Act 1957 by inserting "mineral-bearing land" after "regulation of mines," effectively extending Union control to broader areas of land bearing minerals — which critics say encroaches on the State List subject of land. A new Section 9D prohibits states from levying new taxes on minerals and from collecting outstanding taxes upheld by court orders. For Odisha, which accounts for 43.49% of India's mineral output (valued at ₹67,955 crore in 2024-25) and which stood to collect over ₹1 lakh crore in retrospective dues from miners from April 2026 following a Supreme Court judgment, the amendment effectively nullifies that revenue. Opposition parties allege the BJP's own MPs did not resist the amendment despite Odisha being a BJP-governed state. Tribal communities living on mineral-bearing lands — who bear environmental and health costs of mining — will see welfare programmes further constrained.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>MMDR Act, 1957:</strong> The parent legislation governing mining in India. Under Entry 54 of the Union List, Parliament regulates mines and minerals of national importance. States retain concurrent jurisdiction over minor minerals (Entry 23 of State List) and collect royalties, which form a critical revenue stream for mineral-rich states like Odisha, Chhattisgarh, and Rajasthan.</li>
<li><strong>Royalties and Cesses in Mining:</strong> States collect royalties on minerals (rates fixed by the Centre under the MMDR Act) and had separately levied cesses (like Odisha's ORISED cess) for local development. The Supreme Court struck down ORISED in the Orissa HC, and the Amendment now bars future similar levies, removing a key welfare financing mechanism for mining-affected tribal communities.</li>
<li><strong>Seventh Schedule — Concurrent Jurisdiction Issue:</strong> "Land" is a State List subject (Entry 18), but "mines and minerals" are a Concurrent/Union List subject. The MMDR 2026 Amendment's expansion of "mineral-bearing land" into Union jurisdiction blurs this line and is likely to face constitutional challenge on grounds that it bypasses the State's land authority.</li>
<li><strong>Supreme Court Ruling on ORISED (2025–26):</strong> Odisha's ORISED Act 2004 was challenged by miners in the Orissa HC, which struck it down. The State appealed to the SC, which ruled in its favour — making Odisha eligible to recover retrospective dues since 2005, potentially over ₹1 lakh crore. The MMDR Amendment, by inserting Section 9D, effectively nullifies this recovery.</li>
<li><strong>PM-KUSUM and Mining Royalties:</strong> Mining royalties are critical to state revenues. Odisha's mining revenue accounts for 37.59% of its total projected state revenue in 2026-27. Such fiscal dependence creates structural vulnerability when Centre-mandated royalty rates and now tax prohibitions constrain state revenue autonomy.</li>
</ul>
</div>

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Defence Minister Rajnath Singh presided virtually over the Bhumi Pujan for three expansion projects at Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers (GRSE) — at its Kidderpore, Shalimar, and Raichak facilities — and two projects at Yantra India Ltd.'s (YIL) Metal and Steel Factory in Ishapore. Rajnath Singh invoked Operation Sindoor as evidence that self-reliance in defence is a "force multiplier for battlefield capability" and not merely a policy goal. **Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">

It is one of four major defence public sector undertakings (DPSUs) in the shipbuilding space alongside Mazagon Dock, Cochin Shipyard, and Hindustan Shipyard.</li>
<li><strong>Atmanirbhar Bharat in Defence:</strong> The government has set a target of 70% domestic procurement in defence by 2027. It has published two Positive Indigenisation Lists (now extended to a fourth) that mandate domestic sourcing of specified weapons and platforms. Defence exports have grown from ₹1,521 crore (2016-17) to over ₹21,000 crore (2023-24).</li>
<li><strong>Maritime India Vision 2030:</strong> Launched in 2021 under the Sagarmala Programme umbrella, it aims to grow the maritime economy, increase India's shipbuilding market share from 0.06% to a significant global share, and develop 23 waterways. It has a target of building 1,000+ vessels in Indian shipyards by 2030.</li>
<li><strong>Operation Sindoor (referenced by Rajnath):</strong> Referenced as a recent military operation that demonstrated the value of indigenous weapons and platforms — underscoring the strategic rationale for defence self-reliance beyond cost considerations.</li>
<li><strong>Yantra India Ltd. (YIL):</strong> A DPSU (formerly Ordnance Factories Board entity) under MoD that manufactures precision engineering products, metal components, and ammunition. The Ishapore Metal and Steel Factory is one of YIL's oldest facilities, tracing its lineage to the Rifle Factory Ishapore (1904).</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  SC committee pulls up Assam government for inaction over mining near Kaziranga
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Environment</span>
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Governance</span>

</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper III → Environment → Wildlife protection; National Parks; Eco-Sensitive Zones; mining vs conservation conflict
- GS Paper II → Governance → Judicial oversight; Central Empowered Committee; compliance with SC orders

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
The Central Empowered Committee (CEC), a Supreme Court-constituted body, has pulled up the Assam government for failing to address mining activity near the Parkup Pahar Range — a declared wildlife sanctuary and ecological corridor on Kaziranga National Park's southern boundary. The CEC's strictures follow an RTI filed by activist Rohit Choudhury in June 2026 seeking compliance details on the CEC's May 2025 directions to the Assam Chief Secretary. The CEC found that the comprehensive Watershed Drainage Analysis Report — due by October 2025 — has not been submitted, and that quarterly status reports on action against violations have also not been furnished. The CEC had previously (March 2026) noted mining continued "on one pretext or the other" and that the Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council (KAAC) was presenting an incomplete picture to courts. The controversy coincides with a separate plan to reduce Kaziranga's Eco-Sensitive Zone, which has triggered additional criticism from conservationists.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Central Empowered Committee (CEC):</strong> Constituted by the Supreme Court in 2002 following the T.N. Godavarman case (1995) to monitor forest and wildlife-related compliance. It examines proposals for diversion of forest land, reviews violations, and submits reports/recommendations to the court. Its orders carry the authority of SC directions.</li>
<li><strong>Kaziranga National Park:</strong> A UNESCO World Heritage Site (1985) in Assam, home to two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinoceroses (Indian Rhinoceros — Rhinoceros unicornis), as well as tigers, elephants, and wild water buffaloes. <li><strong>Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ):</strong> Buffer zones around Protected Areas notified under the Environment Protection Act 1986, where specified activities (quarrying, mining, large-scale construction) are regulated or prohibited. <li><strong>Wildlife Protection Act, 1972:</strong> The primary legislation for wildlife conservation, establishing National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Tiger Reserves. <li><strong>SC Order of April 2019 on Kaziranga:</strong> The Supreme Court prohibited all mining and construction in the catchments of rivers and streams originating in the Karbi Anglong hills and flowing into Kaziranga. <h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  Global space norms find a firm footing in India's new re-entry rules — IN-SPACe guidelines
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#F0EBF6;color:#5B2C8D;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Space</span>
  <span style="background:#F0EBF6;color:#5B2C8D;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">AI</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 7 · Science</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper III → Science & Technology → Space governance; space debris; planned re-entry; IN-SPACe regulations; Outer Space Treaty
- GS Paper II → International Relations → Space law; Liability Convention 1972; UN COPUOS

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Days after Skyroot's Vikram-1 orbital success, IN-SPACe released India's first guidelines for planned re-entry of spacecraft — converting international "soft law" into binding domestic obligations for private operators. The guidelines have three pillars: (i) Accountability — any Indian entity undertaking a planned re-entry needs IN-SPACe authorisation; non-Indian entities must route activity through an Indian-incorporated entity; (ii) Risk threshold — the expected casualty risk must remain below 1 in 10,000, requiring operators to submit survivability and fragmentation analyses; and (iii) Permissions — IN-SPACe re-verifies parameters three months before the proposed operation, and operators must issue NOTAM-type warnings to airborne and maritime vessels at least 45 days beforehand. The guidelines also operationalise India's obligations under the Space Liability Convention 1972, requiring operators to indemnify the Government of India for third-party liability claims arising under India's international commitments, while maintaining their own third-party insurance.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Outer Space Treaty 1967 (Article IX):</strong> The foundational UN treaty governing space activities, ratified by over 110 countries including India. It establishes that space is the "province of all mankind," prohibits nuclear weapons in space, and under Article IX requires states to conduct space activities with "due regard" to the interests of others — the basis for environmental responsibility norms.</li>
<li><strong>Space Liability Convention 1972:</strong> Establishes absolute liability of a "launching state" for damage caused by its space object on Earth's surface or to aircraft in flight. India is a party. The Convention applies to both government and private launches, meaning the Government of India bears international liability for damage caused by any spacecraft launched from Indian territory or by an Indian operator.</li>
<li><strong>UN COPUOS Guidelines for Long-term Sustainability of Outer Space Activities (2019):</strong> Adopted by the UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space, these are voluntary guidelines covering safe spacecraft design, debris mitigation, and re-entry. Most are soft law — operators are not legally bound to follow them. The IN-SPACe guidelines convert some of these principles into enforceable domestic obligations.</li>
<li><strong>Space Debris and Re-entry Risks:</strong> Low Earth Orbit (LEO) hosts over 10,000 tracked objects and millions of pieces of debris. With mega-constellations (SpaceX Starlink, Amazon Kuiper) adding thousands of satellites, controlled re-entry ("post-mission disposal") has become critical for orbital sustainability. The Kessler Syndrome — a cascade of collisions creating ever-more debris — is the long-term risk.</li>
<li><strong>Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC):</strong> An international forum of 13 space agencies (including ISRO) that formulates consensus debris mitigation guidelines. The "25-year rule" — requiring satellites to de-orbit within 25 years of end-of-life — is an IADC guideline increasingly adopted domestically, including now by India's IN-SPACe framework.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  Triple Test, Adrift — SC overrules BWSSB judgment for IRC without sound reasoning
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Polity</span>
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Labour</span>
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Social Justice</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 8 · Editorial</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → Indian Polity → Judiciary; Supreme Court; Labour legislation; Industrial Disputes Act; Industrial Relations Code 2020
- GS Paper II → Governance → Workers' rights; definition of "industry"; gig economy; labour reforms

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
On 20 August 2026, a nine-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court, led by CJI Surya Kant, examined the correctness of the 1978 BWSSB vs A. Rajappa ruling, which had established Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer's "Triple Test" for defining an "industry" under Section 2(j) of the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 — requiring a systematic activity, employer-employee cooperation, and production of goods/services satisfying human wants, with profit motive being irrelevant. The Bench left the Triple Test standing for pending disputes under the old ID Act (repealed in November 2025 when the Industrial Relations Code 2020 came into force), but by majority ruled that the BWSSB judgment would not serve as a "sheet anchor" for interpreting Section 2(p) of the IRC 2020. The Hindu's editorial argues this is a mistake, noting that Section 2(p) of the IRC substantially reproduces the Triple Test's essence, making the majority's severance of BWSSB logically inconsistent. Justice B.V. Nagarathna's minority view — that the reference was unnecessary and the Triple Test should remain intact — is commended.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>BWSSB vs A. Rajappa (1978):</strong> A landmark Supreme Court judgment that broadly defined "industry" under the ID Act 1947, bringing charitable organisations, hospitals, educational institutions, and government departments (except sovereign functions) within the ambit of industrial law. The "Triple Test" was: systematic activity + employer-employee cooperation + production of goods/services. This enabled workers in a wide range of establishments to invoke collective bargaining and retrenchment protections.</li>
<li><strong>Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (IRC):</strong> One of four Labour Codes that consolidated 29 central labour laws, came into force on 21 November 2025. It amalgamates the Industrial Disputes Act 1947, the Trade Unions Act 1926, and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946. Section 2(p) provides a definition of "industry" that is closely modelled on the Triple Test.</li>
<li><strong>Four Labour Codes:</strong> The Code on Wages (2019), Industrial Relations Code (2020), Code on Social Security (2020), and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code (2020) — together consolidating 29 central labour laws. States must still enact parallel rules for the Codes to be applicable in their jurisdictions.</li>
<li><strong>Sovereign Functions Exception:</strong> Under BWSSB, activities that are purely a "sovereign function" of the state (like defence, judicial, legislative functions) were excluded from the definition of "industry." This exception has been contested — particularly for departments like water supply boards — and is one reason the reference was made to the nine-judge Bench.</li>
<li><strong>Gig and Platform Workers:</strong> The Industrial Relations Code 2020 does not clearly include gig/platform workers (e.g. food delivery, cab aggregators) within the traditional employer-employee framework. The Code on Social Security 2020 defines "gig workers" and "platform workers" separately, making them eligible for limited social security benefits, but not for collective bargaining under IRC — a significant gap highlighted by labour economists.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background:#1E2D3D;color:#fff;padding:12px 18px;border-radius:7px 7px 0 0;margin:28px 0 0 0;font-size:1.05em;">
  The rural-urban divide in female labour force participation — PLFS 2025 analysis
</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">
  <span style="background:#FFF0E6;color:#B84020;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Women</span>
  <span style="background:#E8F2E8;color:#1A5C2A;font-size:0.72em;font-weight:700;padding:2px 9px;border-radius:3px;margin-right:4px;">Economy</span>
  <span style="color:#8A8278;font-size:0.9em;margin-left:8px;">Page 9 · Opinion</span>
</p>

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper II → Social Justice → Women's empowerment; female labour force participation; gender economic gap
- GS Paper III → Economy → Employment; PLFS data; rural-urban divide; inclusive growth

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) 2025 shows female labour force participation rate (FLFPR) for ages 15+ rose from 30% in 2019-20 to 40% in 2025 — a significant improvement, but one with a sharp rural-urban divergence. Rural FLFPR rose from 33% to 45.9%, while urban FLFPR rose more modestly from 23.3% to 27.7%. The rural surge is partly driven by increased agricultural and self-employment activity post-COVID, including MGNREGS participation, and may reflect distress-driven rather than purely opportunity-driven participation. States like West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, and Odisha recorded above-average annual percentage point (AAPP) gains in rural areas. However, states that started with higher baseline FLFPR (southern states, Goa, Punjab in urban areas) showed slower improvement — suggesting gains are concentrated at the lower end. The urban FLFPR of 27.7% remains well below ILO benchmarks, indicating structural barriers — education gaps, safety concerns, social norms, and care economy burdens — persist.
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS):</strong> Launched in 2017 by the National Statistical Office (NSO/MoSPI) to provide quarterly urban employment data and annual rural+urban employment estimates. It replaced the quinquennial NSSO Employment-Unemployment Surveys with more frequent, disaggregated data on employment, wages, and LFPR.</li>
<li><strong>Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR):</strong> The proportion of a population that is either employed or actively seeking employment, expressed as a percentage of the working-age population. India's overall LFPR (~55%) is comparable to global averages, but the female LFPR gap is among the widest in the world, reflecting social, structural, and policy barriers.</li>
<li><strong>MGNREGS and Female Employment:</strong> The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 mandates at least one-third of beneficiaries be women. In practice, female participation has often exceeded 50% nationally, making MGNREGS a significant driver of rural female employment and wages. It may partially explain the post-COVID rural FLFPR surge.</li>
<li><strong>Distress-Driven vs Opportunity-Driven Participation:</strong> Economists distinguish between women entering the labour force due to economic compulsion (e.g., male unemployment, household income shocks) and those who enter due to expanded opportunities (skill development, structural transformation). The rural surge post-COVID warrants analysis to determine whether it reflects genuine economic empowerment.</li>
<li><strong>Care Economy and Female LFPR:</strong> The burden of unpaid care work (childcare, eldercare, domestic labour) disproportionately falls on women, acting as a structural barrier to formal employment. WHO and ILO estimate women globally perform 76.2% of unpaid care work. Policies like affordable childcare, paid parental leave, and eldercare support systems are key enablers of sustained female LFPR improvement.</li>
</ul>
</div>

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Rajya Sabha MP and lawyer Haris Beeran analyses the Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill 2026, which gave Vande Mataram the same criminal-law protection as Jana Gana Mana — up to three years imprisonment for intentionally preventing or disrupting its singing. The article makes three key legal arguments: (i) Vande Mataram has never been the national anthem or co-anthem — its status rests solely on a Presidential statement of 24 January 1950, not the Constitution; (ii) the 1937 Congress Working Committee compromise limited official renditions to the first two (secular) stanzas, keeping the later devotional stanzas invoking Hindu goddesses Durga, Lakshmi and Saraswati out of civic life; and (iii) the Amendment, correctly read, only penalises disruption of a performance already underway — it does not compel participation. **Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Bijoe Emmanuel & Ors. vs State of Kerala (1986):</strong> A landmark Supreme Court judgment (Justice O. Chinnappa Reddy) that held that Jehovah's Witness children could not be expelled for standing silently during Jana Gana Mana — their faith forbade worship of anyone other than God. The Court ruled that Article 51A's fundamental duty to respect national symbols cannot override fundamental rights under Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of expression, including silence) and Article 25 (freedom of conscience). This ruling directly applies to Vande Mataram.</li>
<li><strong>Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971:</strong> The parent Act that penalises insults to the Indian flag, the Constitution, and the national anthem. The 2026 Amendment extends criminal protection to Vande Mataram. The Act was originally enacted to address growing disrespect to national symbols in the post-1971 war nationalist context.</li>
<li><strong>Vande Mataram — Historical and Constitutional Status:</strong> Written by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay in his 1882 novel Anandamath, the song became the rallying cry of the freedom movement. The first two stanzas are secular-pastoral; later stanzas invoke the motherland as Hindu goddesses. The 1937 Congress Working Committee compromise (including with Muslim League) limited official rendition to stanzas 1 and 2. Dr. Rajendra Prasad's 24 January 1950 statement gave it "equal status" with Jana Gana Mana — but not constitutional status as anthem.</li>
<li><strong>Article 25 and 26 (Freedom of Religion):</strong> Article 25 guarantees freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise, and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality, and health. Article 26 allows religious denominations to manage their own religious affairs. These are the constitutional bulwarks for conscientious objectors to devotional songs in civic settings.</li>
<li><strong>Monsoon Session 2026 — Legislative Passage:</strong> The Prevention of Insults Amendment Bill was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 24 July 2026 and cleared both Houses within a week (RS on 29 July, LS on 30 July), one of 12 Bills passed with limited debate — including the MMDR Amendment — in a session marked by political controversy and the ongoing CJP protests.</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0;padding:4px 18px 6px 18px;background:#f4f6f8;border-radius:0 0 5px 5px;font-size:0.82em;">

**UPSC Mains Classification**
- GS Paper III → Science & Technology → Artificial Intelligence; biotechnology; biosecurity; AI in healthcare
- GS Paper III → Science & Technology → Bacteriophages; antimicrobial resistance; AI-designed pathogens; dual-use research

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
Dr. Abdul Ghafur (Apollo Hospital Chennai) analyses a Stanford University and Arc Institute study in which AI models (Evo 1 and Evo 2 — genome language models analogous to LLMs but trained on DNA sequences) designed complete genomes for bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria). Of 285 AI-generated genomes synthesised and tested in the lab, 16 produced functioning phages — some overcoming bacterial resistance that defeated the original virus. The medical opportunity lies in phage therapy for antibiotic-resistant infections, a growing crisis as AMR threatens to kill 10 million people annually by 2050. The biosecurity concern is "capability amplification" — AI compresses the design-to-experiment cycle, potentially enabling misuse. The article argues India must build its own AI capability for biomedical research (through the IndiaAI Mission and indigenous foundation models) to avoid scientific dependence, while also developing biosafety governance (AI model guardrails + DNA synthesis screening for biological function, not just sequence similarity).
</div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Bacteriophages (Phages):</strong> Viruses that infect and kill bacteria, making them promising therapeutic agents in the post-antibiotic era. Phage therapy was used in Eastern Europe (especially Georgia and Poland) throughout the 20th century. Western medicine is revisiting it as antibiotic resistance worsens. They are highly specific — one phage kills one bacterial strain, limiting broad-spectrum application.</li>
<li><strong>Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):</strong> AMR occurs when bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites evolve to resist the medicines designed to kill them. India is one of the world's largest consumers of antibiotics, and AMR causes an estimated 700,000 deaths globally each year. The Chennai Declaration (2012) was India's commitment to combat AMR through a national action plan.</li>
<li><strong>Genome Language Models:</strong> AI models that learn patterns in DNA sequences across vast genomic databases, analogous to how large language models (LLMs) learn patterns in text. They can then generate novel genomic sequences. Evo 1 and Evo 2 (Arc Institute/Stanford) are examples trained on millions of DNA sequences, enabling de novo genome design.</li>
<li><strong>IndiaAI Mission:</strong> Launched in 2024 with a budget of ₹10,371 crore (5 years), the mission aims to build India's AI computing infrastructure (10,000+ GPU cluster), support AI start-ups, develop indigenous foundational models, develop AI in healthcare and agriculture, and create a responsible AI governance framework. It is coordinated by MeitY.</li>
<li><strong>Biosafety and Dual-Use Research of Concern (DURC):</strong> International frameworks (Biological Weapons Convention 1972, WHO guidance, US NSABB) require oversight of research that could be misused to enhance pathogen transmissibility or lethality. The gain-of-function influenza research debate (2011-12 H5N1 experiments) is the precedent. AI-designed genomes create a new governance challenge — traditional screening checks sequence similarity to known pathogens but not biological function of novel sequences.</li>
</ul>
</div>

**Summary**
<div style="background:#EDF4F2;border-left:4px solid #4A8A78;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:10px 0 6px 0;">
An ICRIER study titled "Solar as a Third Crop to Augment Farmers' Income" explores agrophotovoltaics (APV/agrivoltaics) — the dual use of the same land for agriculture and solar power generation. Rajasthan's first farmer-owned APV, a 600 kW installation on 3 acres in Kundanpura village, Bassi block, was piloted with farmer Kajodmal Jat. The elevated solar panels allow maize cultivation beneath them; the installation feeds daytime irrigation power for 250 farms. Jat's income rose from ₹40,000/year (farming) to ₹3 lakh/year (APV). The study models two financial scenarios: without Viability Gap Funding (VGF), payback is ~12 years; with VGF, it reduces to ~6 years and ROI rises from 12% to 18%. The key barriers to scaling are: (i) high upfront cost — elevated mounting structures add ₹35 lakh over conventional solar; (ii) no dedicated APV procurement pathway in state tenders; (iii) reluctant DISCOMs hesitant to sign long-term PPAs; (iv) no centralised monitoring or grievance redressal for PM-KUSUM; (v) absence of NABARD credit guarantee for agriPV. </div>

**Background Knowledge**
<div style="background:#F9F3E8;border-left:4px solid #B07030;border-radius:0 5px 5px 0;padding:12px 16px;margin:6px 0 16px 0;">
<ul>
<li><strong>PM-KUSUM (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan, 2019):</strong> A Central government scheme with three components: Component A (standalone solar pumps for farmers), Component B (solarisation of grid-connected agricultural pumps), and Component C (installation of solar power plants on barren/fallow land by farmers, with electricity sold to DISCOMs). It targets 30.8 GW of solar capacity through farmer participation, reducing agricultural dependence on subsidised grid power.</li>
<li><strong>Agrophotovoltaics (APV/Agrivoltaics):</strong> A land-use concept pioneered in Germany and France in the 1980s-2000s, now gaining policy attention globally. Elevated solar panels are mounted 3-5 metres high over cropland; partial shading reduces heat stress and evapotranspiration, benefiting drought-sensitive crops. Germany, France, and Japan have formal APV standards and renewable purchase obligations — India does not yet.</li>
<li><strong>Viability Gap Funding (VGF):</strong> A Central government grant provided to make commercially unviable but socially desirable infrastructure projects financially viable. In the renewable energy context, VGF is used for projects with high upfront costs but long-term social and environmental benefits. Under PM-KUSUM Component A, a subsidy of 30% (Centre) + 30% (State) reduces farmer outlay to 40%.</li>
<li><strong>NABARD and Rural Finance:</strong> The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development provides refinance to rural cooperative banks and RRBs for agricultural and rural development lending. A NABARD credit guarantee mechanism for APV projects — which currently does not exist — would reduce lending risk and enable smaller farmers to access APV financing at lower interest rates.</li>
<li><strong>Global Hunger Index 2025:</strong> India ranked 102nd out of 123 countries, with a "serious" hunger level. This context makes large-scale diversion of productive farmland to ground-mounted solar a food security risk, strengthening the case for APV as a dual-use solution that avoids the food-energy tradeoff inherent in conventional solar parks on agricultural land.</li>
</ul>
</div>
