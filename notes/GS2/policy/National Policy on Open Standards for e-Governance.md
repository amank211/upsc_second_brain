<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Policy on Open Standards for e-Governance</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Provides a framework for selecting open standards to <b>avoid vendor lock-in</b> and develop cost-effective, interoperable e-Governance applications.</li>
<li>Released as part of the broader National e-Governance Plan (NeGP) framework, so government departments' applications can work together without proprietary dependency.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Focus Areas</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Interoperability:</b> standards ensure different e-Governance applications can work with and understand each other.</li>
<li><b>Biometric Standards:</b> a dedicated biometric standard for e-Governance applications lets systems exchange biometric data interoperably.</li>
<li><b>Standardization &amp; Uniformity:</b> ensures uniform accessibility to critical government information and services regardless of which department or state built the application.</li>
<li><b>ICT Integration:</b> supports efficient, speedy, and cost-effective public-service delivery via ICT.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National e-Governance Plan"; "IT-Enabled Transparency".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle — National e-Governance Plan (NeGP) → e-Kranti</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;"><b>NeGP</b>, approved by Cabinet in May 2006, is the umbrella plan the Open Standards Policy serves — envisioning public services delivered to citizens at their locality, affordably.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Comprises <b>27 Mission Mode Projects (MMPs)</b> — 9 Central, 11 State, 7 Integrated — plus 8 core components; 24 MMPs approved, 14 have gone live delivering services electronically.</li>
<li>Of 1,100 targeted services, over 600 are accessible nationwide.</li>
<li><b>Core infrastructure:</b> State Data Centres (SDCs), State Wide Area Networks (SWANs), State e-Governance Service Delivery Gateways (SSDGs), e-District projects, and Capacity Building.</li>
<li><b>Common Services Centres (CSCs):</b> 86,000+ established, providing access to ~60 million people across ~5 lakh villages.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>e-Kranti (Digital India's Pillar 5):</b> subsumed NeGP under Digital India (2015), expanding the MMP portfolio, driving Government Process Reengineering, workflow automation, and cloud/mobile-platform adoption.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National e-Governance Plan"; "Approach and Key Components of e-Kranti: National e-Governance Plan 2.0".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Persistent Digital Divide:</b> only ~24% of rural Indian households have internet access versus ~66% in urban areas (NSSO), limiting e-Governance's actual reach.</li>
<li><b>Weak Interoperability in Practice:</b> despite the Open Standards framework, interoperation among ministries/departments remains difficult — most state/central e-Governance services are still not integrated, and digitization levels vary widely by state.</li>
<li><b>Implementation Gaps:</b> inadequate planning, leadership and financing shortfalls, weak citizen-centric design, and poor bureaucrat-citizen cooperation continue to hamper rollout.</li>
<li><b>Digital &amp; Linguistic Literacy Gaps:</b> despite large-scale programmes like PMGDISHA, rural digital literacy remains low, and most applications default to English/Hindi, excluding much of the linguistically diverse rural population.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "E-Governance in India, Objectives, Pillars, Govt Policies, Challenges"; Drishti IAS — "E-Governance in India".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Enforcing the Open Standards framework more consistently across states, closing the rural connectivity/literacy gap (via BharatNet and PMGDISHA), and building genuinely citizen-centric, multilingual application design are central to converting NeGP/e-Kranti's MMP rollout into truly interoperable, universally accessible e-Governance.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi; Drishti IAS; PIB.</p>
</div>
