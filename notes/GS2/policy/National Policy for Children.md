<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Policy for Children, 1974</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's first National Policy for Children, adopted in 1974, under the Ministry of Women and Child Development's predecessor administrative setup.</li>
<li>Highlighted the need for a comprehensive child welfare programme, framing children as the nation's "supremely important asset" and their care and protection as the nation's responsibility.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Welfare-oriented approach — focused on nutrition, health care, and education as basic services owed to children, rather than a rights-based framework.</li>
<li>Called for a comprehensive, integrated programme of child welfare, spanning nutrition, health, and pre-school informal education, to be delivered before, at, and after birth, through childhood.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Policy for Children"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle Scheme — Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS), 1975</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Launched on <b>2 October 1975</b> as a pilot, ICDS operationalized the 1974 policy's vision and remains the world's largest community-based early childhood development programme, delivered through <b>Anganwadi Centres (AWCs)</b>.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Package of services:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Supplementary nutrition for children (0–6 years), pregnant women, and lactating mothers.</li>
<li>Immunization, health check-ups, and referral services.</li>
<li>Non-formal pre-school (early childhood) education.</li>
<li>Health and nutrition education for mothers.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Integrated Child Development Services, Objectives, Restructuring"; Drishti IAS.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Challenges</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Purely welfare-oriented framing did not treat children as rights-holders, leaving no legal/rights basis to hold the state accountable for gaps in delivery.</li>
<li>Narrow focus on nutrition, health, and early education left out protection, participation, and the needs of adolescents and children in especially difficult circumstances.</li>
<li>Did not anticipate the multi-sectoral, cross-ministry coordination that child welfare would eventually require, contributing to fragmented, scheme-by-scheme delivery over time.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Policy for Children (NPC), 2013</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Adopted by the Ministry of Women and Child Development on <b>26 April 2013</b>, NPC 2013 affirms the Government's commitment to realizing the rights of all children in the country, replacing the welfare-oriented NPC 1974 with a <b>rights-based approach</b>.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;">It recognizes every person below the age of <b>18 years</b> as a child, treats childhood as an integral part of life with a value of its own, and — as a signatory to the <b>UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC)</b> — commits to ensuring every child's right to survival, development, protection, and participation.</p>
</div>

</div>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0; font-size:0.85em; color:#666666;">As children's needs are multi-sectoral and interconnected, the policy calls for purposeful convergence and coordination across ministries, states, and district-level authorities.</p>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.2. Vehicle Schemes</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Survival, Health &amp; Nutrition — Poshan Abhiyaan → Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Poshan Abhiyaan (National Nutrition Mission), 2018:</b> multi-ministerial convergence mission placing nutrition at the centre of the development agenda, with time-bound targets and digital monitoring.</li>
<li><b>Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 (2021-22):</b> the current umbrella Integrated Nutrition Support Programme, merging ICDS, Poshan Abhiyaan, the Scheme for Adolescent Girls, and the National Crèche Scheme. Provides supplementary nutrition, Early Childhood Care &amp; Education (ECCE) for ages 3–6 with early stimulation for 0–3, and upgraded "Saksham" Anganwadi infrastructure (LED screens, water purifiers, Poshan Vatikas).</li>
<li><b>Poshan Tracker</b> (launched 1 March 2021): digital governance tool for real-time monitoring of Anganwadi service delivery, attendance, and growth monitoring.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; padding-top:10px; border-top:1px dashed #a8d5b0; font-size:0.85em; color:#2c5f3d;"><b>Related but distinct — Mid-Day Meal Scheme (PM POSHAN):</b> covers the <b>next age band</b> — school-age children (primary/upper primary) — with a cooked school meal. Run by the <b>Ministry of Education</b>, not WCD, so it sits outside ICDS/Saksham Anganwadi; it traces instead to NPE 1986/92 (see [[Policy on Education]]). Together with ICDS/Poshan 2.0, it covers the nutrition continuum from Anganwadi to school.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Protection &amp; Participation — Mission Vatsalya</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Implemented as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme since 2009-10 (originally the <b>Integrated Child Protection Scheme, ICPS</b>), renamed and revamped as <b>Mission Vatsalya</b> in 2021-22.</li>
<li>Delivers institutional and non-institutional care services to <b>Children in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP)</b> and <b>Children in Conflict with Law (CCL)</b>, including Child Care Institutions offering education, vocational training, health care, and counselling.</li>
<li>Backed by a dedicated legal framework: the <b>Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015</b> and the <b>Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012</b>, with the statutory <b>National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR)</b> monitoring implementation.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Mission Vatsalya scheme deliver services for Children in Need of Care and Protection..."; "Schemes implemented by the Ministry of Women and Child Development clubbed into three verticals"; Drishti IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.5. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Persistent Malnutrition:</b> Despite steady improvement, more than a third of under-5 children remain stunted and nearly a fifth wasted — well short of the policy's survival-and-nutrition goals.</li>
<li><b>Child Labour Enforcement Gaps:</b> Comprehensive laws exist, but a shortage of labour inspectors and the prevalence of informal-sector employment make monitoring and prosecution difficult; schemes like the National Child Labour Project (1988) remain constrained by insufficient funding.</li>
<li><b>Child Trafficking:</b> No comprehensive anti-trafficking programme, an absent witness-protection framework, and difficulty accessing compensation — NCRB data show roughly eight children trafficked every day in India in 2021, for labour, begging, and sexual exploitation.</li>
<li><b>Fragmented, Multi-Scheme Delivery:</b> Convergence across nutrition (Saksham Anganwadi/Poshan 2.0), protection (Mission Vatsalya), and education remains a coordination challenge across ministries, states, and districts.</li>
<li><b>Anganwadi Worker Capacity:</b> Anganwadi workers/helpers remain underpaid and overburdened relative to the widening scope of services (ECCE, NCD-adjacent screening, digital data entry) now routed through them.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Child Trafficking in India"; Drishti IAS; PIB — "Child Labour Laws".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Closing the gap between NPC 2013's rights-based promise and ground realities depends on tighter convergence between the nutrition (Saksham Anganwadi/Poshan 2.0) and protection (Mission Vatsalya) verticals, stronger enforcement of child labour and trafficking laws, and adequately resourcing the Anganwadi workforce that ultimately delivers both.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB; Drishti IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>
