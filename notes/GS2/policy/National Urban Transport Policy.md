<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Urban Transport Policy (NUTP), 2006</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Aims at better mobility and sustainability by focusing on <b>moving people, not vehicles</b> — a fundamental shift from vehicle-centric to people-centric urban transport planning.</li>
<li>Envisages safe, affordable, quick, comfortable, reliable, and sustainable urban transport systems.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Establishes quality-focused, well-integrated <b>multi-modal public transport systems</b>.</li>
<li>Promotes public transport and non-motorized transport options, within a framework contextual to each city's geography, society, and economy.</li>
<li>Emphasizes strengthening state/city-level capacity to plan and manage sustainable urban transport networks.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Urban Transport Policy".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle Schemes</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Metro Rail Expansion</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">India now has over <b>1,000 km</b> of operational metro rail network — the world's <b>3rd-largest</b> metro system after China and the US.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Pre-2014: only 5 cities, 229 km of metro. By April 2023: 860 km across 20 cities.</li>
<li>Pace of commissioning rose from 0.68 km/month (pre-May 2014) to <b>5.6 km/month</b> (April 2023).</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>PM-eBus Sewa Scheme, 2023</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Deploys <b>10,000 electric buses</b> across 169 cities (population 3–40 lakh, plus select smaller State capitals and NE/hilly capitals) — total estimated cost <b>₹57,613 crore</b>, with ₹20,000 crore Central support, backing bus operations for 10 years.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>As of Feb 2026: tenders concluded for 6,228 buses; Letters of Award issued for 4,720 buses.</li>
<li>~30% of participating cities get an organized public bus system for the first time; ~80% transition to e-mobility for the first time.</li>
<li>Generates an estimated 45,000–55,000 direct jobs.</li>
<li><b>National Common Mobility Card (NCMC)</b> — "One Nation, One Card" — functional across 11 metro projects and 11 bus corporations for seamless multi-modal travel.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "PM-eBus Sewa"; "India's Metro Revolution: From Miles to Milestones".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Persistent Congestion:</b> commuters in metropolitan cities still spend 1.5–2 hours daily in traffic despite metro/bus expansion.</li>
<li><b>Last-Mile Connectivity Gap:</b> metro stations often lack adequate bus connectivity or last-mile solutions to final destinations, discouraging public-transport use even where trunk infrastructure exists.</li>
<li><b>Operational Inefficiencies:</b> inconsistent service frequencies and inadequate peak-hour crowd management undercut the reliability NUTP 2006 envisioned.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS — "Crowding in Public Transport: India's Urban Transit Dynamics"; "Sustainable Urban Mobility in India"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Transport and Communication, Government Initiatives, Challenges".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Closing last-mile gaps between metro/bus trunk routes and final destinations, improving service-frequency reliability, and expanding NCMC-style integrated ticketing are central to converting India's metro/e-bus infrastructure gains into the "moving people, not vehicles" outcome NUTP 2006 set out to achieve.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi; PIB.</p>
</div>
