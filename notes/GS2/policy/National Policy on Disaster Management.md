<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. Disaster Management Act, 2005 — Legislative Backbone</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Provides a comprehensive legal and institutional framework for disaster prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, relief, rehabilitation, and reconstruction at national, state, and district levels.</li>
<li>Marked a significant shift in India's disaster-management strategy — from a purely response-oriented approach to one centred on preparedness, prevention, and planning.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Disaster Management Act, 2005, Background, Objectives, Key Provisions".</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Policy on Disaster Management (NPDM), 2009</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Approved by the Union Cabinet on <b>22 October 2009</b>, providing a comprehensive framework for disaster risk reduction and response.</li>
<li>Marks a shift from a relief-centric approach to a proactive strategy emphasizing prevention, mitigation, preparedness, and effective response — a holistic, technology-driven, multi-disaster approach.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Objectives &amp; Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Build a safe and disaster-resilient India through a culture of prevention, mitigation, preparedness, and response.</li>
<li>Integrate disaster management into development planning, rather than treating it as a separate post-disaster activity.</li>
<li>Establish institutional and techno-legal frameworks across all levels of government.</li>
<li>Covers the full disaster-management cycle — prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, rehabilitation, reconstruction, and recovery.</li>
<li>Prioritizes equity and inclusiveness, factoring in the concerns of all sections of society in relief and rehabilitation.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Policy on Disaster Management (NPDM)".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.1. Vehicle — National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">India's apex statutory body for disaster management, constituted on <b>27 September 2006</b> under the DM Act 2005, with the <b>Prime Minister as Chairperson</b> and nine other members.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Operates through specialized divisions — Policy &amp; Plans, Mitigation, Operations, Communications &amp; IT, Administration, and Finance.</li>
<li><b>State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs)</b> and <b>District Disaster Management Authorities (DDMAs)</b> function under its overall framework and guidelines.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS — "National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Vehicle — National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>As of 2025, comprises <b>16 battalions</b> strategically located across India, each with 1,149 personnel specializing in search-and-rescue, communication, and medical assistance.</li>
<li>Led major operations: Kosi floods (2008), J&amp;K floods (2014), Nepal earthquake (2015), Balasore train tragedy (2023); international deployments to Japan (2011) and Turkey-Syria (2023).</li>
<li><b>Aapda Mitra</b> scheme: trains 1 lakh volunteers across 350 disaster-prone districts (landslides, cyclones, earthquakes, floods).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "National Disaster Response Force, Roles, Structure, Operations"; PIB.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Funding Misallocation:</b> SDRF/NDRF (relief) and SDRMF/NDRMF (mitigation) allocations are still based on population and area rather than actual hazard risk, with delays and ambiguous "severe disaster" criteria causing inconsistent support relative to real losses.</li>
<li><b>Over-Centralization:</b> disaster management remains reactive and over-centralized, with insufficient functional autonomy, technical capacity, and fund flow reaching District and local levels.</li>
<li><b>Institutional Coordination:</b> the large number of stakeholders (DDMA, SDMA, NDMA, SEC, NEC) with overlapping roles makes inter-institutional coordination difficult.</li>
<li><b>Local Capacity Gaps:</b> many DDMAs and urban local bodies lack staff, GIS capability, digital tools, and planning capacity, hampering implementation even where funds exist.</li>
<li><b>Low Public Awareness:</b> per NDMA's own 2022 estimate, only 30–35% of the rural population is aware of disaster-preparedness measures.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS — "Reforming India's Disaster Strategy"; "Advancing Disaster Resilience in India"; "India's Disaster Risk Financing".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Shifting fund allocation to risk-based criteria, strengthening DDMA/local-body technical capacity, streamlining inter-institutional coordination, and scaling up community awareness programmes like Aapda Mitra are central to closing the gap between NPDM 2009's proactive vision and ground-level preparedness.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS; PIB; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>
