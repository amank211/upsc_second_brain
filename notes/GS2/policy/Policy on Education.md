<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Policy on Education (NPE), 1968</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's first National Policy on Education, based on the recommendations of the Kothari Commission (1964–66).</li>
<li>India's first major education policy framework after independence, laying the groundwork for subsequent reforms.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Prescribed the <b>Three-Language Formula</b> — Hindi, English, and a regional/mother-tongue language.</li>
<li>Focused on core subjects — integrating science, mathematics, social studies, and national service throughout the education journey.</li>
<li>Proposed expanding the number of universities and investment in research and innovation, and emphasized developing vocational and technical institutions to serve the needs of a modern economy.</li>
<li>Called for equalization of educational opportunity across sections of society.</li>
<li>Set the (still-unmet) target of raising public spending on education to <b>6% of GDP</b>.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Policy on Education (NPE), 1986 (modified by Programme of Action, 1992)</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Focused on providing education to all sections of society, promoting primary education, and establishing open universities.</li>
<li>The 1986 policy was modified by the Programme of Action (POA) in 1992 — together referred to as NPE 1986/92 — and remained India's national education framework until replaced by NEP 2020.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Gave rise to major implementing schemes/institutions still in use today: <b>Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan</b>, the <b>Mid-Day Meal Scheme</b>, <b>Navodaya Vidyalayas (NVS)</b>, and <b>Kendriya Vidyalayas (KVs)</b>.</li>
<li>Promoted the use of IT in education.</li>
<li>The <b>Programme of Action, 1992</b> placed special emphasis on early childhood care and education (ECCE), and on universalization of elementary education.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">3. National Education Policy (NEP), 2020</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">NEP 2020, approved by the Union Cabinet in July 2020, is only the third National Policy on Education in independent India, and the first policy of the 21st century. It replaces the NPE 1986/92.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;">It is built on five foundational pillars — <b>Access, Equity, Quality, Affordability, and Accountability</b>.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">3.2. Key Features</h3>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>School Education</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>No rigid separation between arts and sciences, curricular and extra-curricular activities, or vocational and academic streams.</li>
<li>Mother tongue/local/regional language as the medium of instruction until Grade 5, preferably till Grade 8 and beyond.</li>
<li>Vocational education to start from Grade 6, including internships.</li>
<li>GER target: <b>100%</b> from preschool to secondary level by 2030.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>NIPUN Bharat Mission (Foundational Literacy and Numeracy)</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">NIPUN Bharat is NEP 2020's flagship vehicle for achieving universal foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) by Grade 3, promoting joyful, activity-based learning from preschool onward.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Components:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>NIPUN Bharat Documents — official documents guiding the mission.</li>
<li>NIPUN Mission Videos — visual resources for mission understanding.</li>
<li>Regional Workshop Resources — materials for regional training sessions.</li>
<li>Primers in Indian Languages — introductory books in local languages.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Higher Education</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>GER target: from 26.3% (2018) to <b>50% by 2035</b>.</li>
<li>A single overarching umbrella body — the <b>Higher Education Commission of India (HECI)</b> — for the higher education sector (excluding medical and legal education), with four independent verticals:
<ul style="margin:2px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>General Education Council</b> — standard setting.</li>
<li><b>Higher Education Grants Council (HEGC)</b> — funding.</li>
<li><b>National Accreditation Council (NAC)</b> — accreditation.</li>
<li><b>National Higher Education Regulatory Council (NHERC)</b> — regulation.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Current Regulatory Landscape (Pre-HECI)</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">HECI is meant to consolidate the fragmented regulatory landscape that exists today, where multiple bodies govern different parts of higher education:</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>University Grants Commission (UGC).</li>
<li>AICTE, MCI, PCI, DEC, BCI, NCTE (technical, medical, pharmacy, distance, legal, and teacher education regulators respectively).</li>
<li>ICAR, ICMR, ICSSR, CSIR (agriculture, medical, social science, and scientific research bodies).</li>
<li>State Regulators.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Financing &amp; Research: HEFA and NRF</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Higher Education Financing Agency (HEFA):</b> Launched in 2017 (pre-dates NEP 2020, but aligned with its vision), providing low-interest loans for infrastructure and research facilities. By March 2025, HEFA had sanctioned over ₹43,438 crore across 109 institutions.</li>
<li><b>National Research Foundation (NRF):</b> NEP 2020 proposed an NRF to fund and mentor quality research. This was operationalized via the <b>NRF Act, 2023</b> as the <b>Anusandhan National Research Foundation</b>, replacing the Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB), with a ₹50,000 crore budget for 2023–28 (28% public, 72% private/philanthropy).</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Teacher Education, Assessment &amp; Equity</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>The existing B.Ed. programme to be replaced by a <b>4-year integrated programme</b> with high-quality content, pedagogy, and practical training.</li>
<li>A new National Assessment Centre, <b>PARAKH</b> (Performance Assessment, Review, and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic Development).</li>
<li>Special emphasis on Socially and Economically Disadvantaged Groups (SEDGs); a separate <b>Gender Inclusion Fund</b> and <b>Special Education Zones</b> for disadvantaged regions and groups.</li>
<li>Centre and States to work together to raise public investment in education to <b>6% of GDP</b> at the earliest.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Salient Features of NEP, 2020" / "Highlights of New Education Policy-2020"; Social Justice note (HEFA, NRF figures).</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">3.5. Challenges NEP 2020 Faces, 5 Years In</h3>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Funding:</b> Public spending on education remains below NEP's 6% of GDP target, with budgetary allocations falling short of the policy's ambitious reforms — a gap unmet since 1968.</li>
<li><b>Centre-State Disagreements:</b> Centre-state disagreements, lack of funds, and delayed teacher education reforms are slowing implementation.</li>
<li><b>Language Policy Friction:</b> Tamil Nadu, which follows a Tamil-English model, views the three-language formula as a covert attempt to impose Hindi and has rejected the proposal outright.</li>
<li><b>Unfinished Institutional Reforms:</b> The Education Ministry is still drafting the HECI bill to replace the UGC for regulating higher education.</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">With strategic investment, inter-governmental coordination, and innovation, NEP's goals of a flexible, inclusive, and future-ready education ecosystem can be achieved.</p>
