<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Sports Policy (NSP), 2001</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Guided by twin objectives: <b>broad-basing of sports</b> (mass participation) and <b>promotion of excellence</b>.</li>
<li>A comprehensive revision was drafted in 2007-08 with stakeholders (State Governments, IOA, NSFs, athletes) but was ultimately withdrawn — the Government judged NSP 2001 still sufficient, leaving it in force until 2025.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Upgradation and development of sports infrastructure; support to National Sports Federations (NSFs) and other sports bodies.</li>
<li>Strengthening scientific and coaching support; enhanced participation of women, Scheduled Tribes, and rural youth.</li>
<li>Involvement of the corporate sector in sports promotion; promoting sports-mindedness among the public.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Status of implementation of National Sports Policy".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle Schemes</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Khelo India, 2017</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">A 12-vertical programme infusing sports culture and pursuing excellence, covering "sports for all" across gender and age groups.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>1,045 Khelo India Centres (KICs)</b> for grassroots training; 34 Khelo India State Centres of Excellence (KISCEs); 306 accredited academies.</li>
<li>Talented athletes receive ~₹6.28 lakh/year support, including ₹10,000/month for training expenses.</li>
<li>14 medals won by Khelo India athletes at the 31st World University Games.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Target Olympic Podium Scheme (TOPS)</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Supports India's top athletes for Olympic/Paralympic preparation — foreign training, international exposure, specialized coaching, equipment, and a ₹50,000/month stipend.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;">Contributed to India's medal performances at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024; India's Paralympic medal tally rose from <b>4 (Rio 2016)</b> to <b>19 (Tokyo 2020)</b> to <b>29 (Paris 2024)</b>.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Khelo India – National Programme for Development of Sports"; "India's Paralympic Story".</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Sports Policy 2025 ("Khelo Bharat Niti")</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Approved by the Union Cabinet, replacing NSP 2001 — a roadmap to make India a <b>global sporting powerhouse</b>, with the <b>2036 Olympics</b> as a focal target.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Five Pillars:</b> Talent Development; Infrastructure &amp; Competitions; Support Systems (world-class training/coaching, stronger NSF governance); Science &amp; Technology; Social Development (marginalized-group inclusion, traditional/indigenous games, diaspora engagement).</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Cabinet approves National Sports Policy 2025"; Vision IAS.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.1. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Uneven Grassroots Infrastructure:</b> since sport is a State subject, there is no uniform national approach to infrastructure development — rural areas especially suffer from poorly maintained facilities and limited equipment.</li>
<li><b>Governance Failures:</b> politicisation, red tape, and lack of professionalism have produced high-profile crises — the Wrestling Federation of India sexual-harassment case (2023) and the IOC's suspension of the Indian Olympic Association (2022).</li>
<li><b>Volunteer-Run Federations:</b> many sports governing bodies still rely on volunteers rather than professional staff, ill-suited to a commercialized, professionalized sporting sector.</li>
<li><b>Short-Termism:</b> a focus on celebrating individual achievements has historically overshadowed sustained investment in grassroots development.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS — "Sports Governance in India"; "Empowering India Through Sports".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">The <b>National Sports Governance Act (NSGA), 2025</b> (enacted 18 August 2025) directly targets federation-governance failures, while NSP 2025's push for innovative financing (PPPs, CSR) aims to widen the funding base beyond government grants — both central to sustaining Khelo India/TOPS gains through to the 2036 Olympics.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "National Sports Governance Bill 2025"; Drishti IAS; PIB.</p>
</div>
