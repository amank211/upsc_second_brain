<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. Legislative Backbone — Child Labour (Prohibition &amp; Regulation) Act, 1986</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Prohibited the employment of children below 14 years in specified hazardous occupations and processes, and regulated conditions of work in others.</li>
<li>Provided the legal foundation on which the National Policy on Child Labour, 1987 built its "Legislative Action Plan" component.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986, Background, Impact".</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Policy on Child Labour, 1987</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's dedicated policy framework for the eradication of child labour, adopted in 1987 and still in force.</li>
<li>Takes a holistic approach — combining legal prohibition, rehabilitation of rescued children, and welfare/development programmes for their families — rather than legal prohibition alone.</li>
<li>In practice, focuses more heavily on <b>rehabilitation</b> of children found in hazardous occupations than on upstream prevention.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Multi-Pronged Strategy</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Legislative Action Plan:</b> strict enforcement of the Child Labour (Prohibition &amp; Regulation) Act, 1986 and other labour laws.</li>
<li><b>Project-Based Plan of Action:</b> project-based rehabilitation in areas of high child-labour concentration — operationalized as the National Child Labour Project (NCLP).</li>
<li><b>General Development Programmes:</b> converging poverty-alleviation and employment-generation schemes to benefit the families of child labourers, addressing the poverty that drives children into work.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB; Drishti IAS.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.1. Vehicle Schemes</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>National Child Labour Project (NCLP), 1988</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Implemented by the Ministry of Labour &amp; Employment through District Project Societies (chaired by the District Magistrate) — the policy's primary rehabilitation vehicle.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Children aged 9–14 rescued/withdrawn from work are enrolled in <b>NCLP Special Training Centres (STCs)</b>.</li>
<li>STCs provide bridge/non-formal education, vocational training, mid-day meals, a stipend, and health care, before mainstreaming children into formal schooling.</li>
<li>Around <b>14 lakh child labourers</b> rescued, rehabilitated, and mainstreamed under NCLP since its inception in 1988.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "The National Child Labour Project (NCLP) Scheme"; "Rehabilitation of Child Labourers".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Child Labour (Prohibition &amp; Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">In force from 1 September 2016; renamed the parent Act to the <b>Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act</b>.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Complete prohibition of work or employment of children below <b>14 years</b> in <b>any</b> occupation or process (not just hazardous ones).</li>
<li>Adolescents aged <b>14–18</b> prohibited from working in hazardous occupations and processes specifically.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>PENCIL Portal, 2017</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">"Platform for Effective Enforcement for No Child Labour" — launched 26 September 2017 by the Ministry of Labour &amp; Employment, digitizing both enforcement of the 1986/2016 Act and implementation of the NCLP Scheme.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Five components:</b> Complaint Corner, Child &amp; Adolescent Labour Tracking System, State Government, National Child Labour Project, and Convergence (linking with other child-welfare schemes).</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "PENCIL portal being used for effective enforcement..."; Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "PENCIL Portal".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">2.3. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Rehabilitation-Heavy, Prevention-Light:</b> The policy's own design leans towards rehabilitating children already found working, rather than preventing them from entering work in the first place.</li>
<li><b>Root-Cause Persistence:</b> Poverty, lack of access to education, and illiteracy continue to push families to send children into work — the "General Development Programmes" convergence strategy has not closed this gap.</li>
<li><b>Geographic Concentration:</b> Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra together account for more than half of India's child labourers, with UP alone accounting for almost 20%.</li>
<li><b>Informal/Agricultural Sector:</b> Most child labourers work as cultivators or agricultural labourers in the informal economy, where labour inspection and enforcement are weakest.</li>
<li><b>Compounding Illiteracy:</b> About 1.4 million child labourers aged 7–14 cannot even write their name — roughly one in three child labourers in that age group is illiterate, undermining the rehabilitate-and-mainstream model.</li>
<li><b>Enforcement Gaps:</b> A shortage of labour inspectors relative to the scale of informal employment limits the practical reach of the 1986/2016 Act despite its stronger legal prohibitions.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS — "Rise in Child Labour" / "Child Labour In India"; PIB.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Shifting the policy's centre of gravity from rehabilitation towards prevention — by tightening convergence between NCLP/PENCIL and poverty-alleviation and school-retention schemes, and strengthening labour inspection in the informal and agricultural sectors — remains key to closing the gap between the 1986/2016 Act's legal prohibitions and ground realities.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS; PIB; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>
