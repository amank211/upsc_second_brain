<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Health Policy (NHP), 1983</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's first National Health Policy, framed in line with the Alma-Ata Declaration (1978) commitment to "Health for All by the year 2000" through primary health care.</li>
<li>Took a needs-based approach, aiming to provide comprehensive primary health care at an affordable cost, rather than a purely curative, hospital-centric model.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Called for a decentralized, well-dispersed network of comprehensive primary health care services, backed by a well-worked-out referral system linking PHCs to secondary and tertiary care.</li>
<li>Prioritized rural health infrastructure and the control of communicable diseases, and of maternal and child health conditions.</li>
<li>Sought to intermesh the services and support of the private health sector with governmental efforts in an integrated manner.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Challenges</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Health agendas and resultant policies were often not customized to the requirements of individual states, which were as divergent in their population and health profiles as in geography and culture — a one-size-fits-all approach on a highly federal subject.</li>
<li>Did not anticipate India's coming demographic and epidemiological transition, which eventually necessitated a policy revision.</li>
<li>Lacked a comprehensive approach to key Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease, with limited emphasis on health promotion and preventive measures to reduce exposure to risk factors.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. National Health Policy (NHP), 2002</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Drafted to address the gaps and fallouts of NHP 1983, with the objective of ensuring more equitable access to health services across the social and geographical expanse of the country.</li>
<li>Emphasized increasing the utilization of public health care facilities and, to widen service delivery, encouraged private investment in health care.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Financing Targets (by 2010)</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Raise overall health sector spending to <b>6% of GDP</b>, of which public health investment was to reach <b>2% of GDP</b>.</li>
<li>Raise the Central Government's contribution to total public health spending from 15% to <b>25%</b>.</li>
<li>Allocate public health investment as <b>55% to primary, 35% to secondary, and 10% to tertiary</b> care.</li>
<li>Raise government-funded health research to 1% of total health spending by 2005, and 2% by 2010.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Other Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Convergence of all vertical health programmes under unified field administration.</li>
<li>Mandatory two-year rural posting for medical graduates, and an expanded practitioner pool including Indian Systems of Medicine (AYUSH) practitioners.</li>
<li>Strengthened panchayat involvement in local health programmes, targeted for 2005.</li>
<li>Operationalization of a disease surveillance network and baseline disease-incidence estimates, both targeted for 2005.</li>
<li>NGO involvement in public health programmes, with a minimum 10% budget allocation.</li>
<li>Promotion of medical tourism as a source of foreign exchange.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB (Highlights of National Health Policy-2002).</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>National Rural Health Mission (NRHM), 2005 → National Health Mission (NHM)</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">NHP 2002's push for rural primary care delivery led to the launch of the <b>NRHM in 2005</b>, which introduced <b>ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist)</b> community health volunteers. In 2013, NRHM merged with the newly launched National Urban Health Mission (NUHM) to form the <b>National Health Mission (NHM)</b> — the umbrella delivery vehicle that NHP 2017 subsequently builds on.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>NHM impact (2014 → 2018-20):</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR): 130 → <b>97 per lakh live births</b> (2014-16 → 2018-20).</li>
<li>Infant Mortality Rate (IMR): 39 → <b>28 per 1,000 live births</b> (2014 → 2020).</li>
<li>Under-5 Mortality Rate (U5MR): 45 → <b>32 per 1,000 live births</b> (2014 → 2020).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vajiram &amp; Ravi; PIB.</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Challenges</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Financing Gap:</b> Actual public health expenditure remained well below the targeted 2% of GDP, leaving several of the policy's own initiatives inadequately funded.</li>
<li><b>Continued NCD Neglect:</b> Still lacked a comprehensive approach to key NCDs (diabetes, cardiovascular disease), with limited facilities and capacity for screening, early diagnosis, and effective management within the public health system.</li>
<li><b>HRH Mismatch:</b> Human-resources-for-health recommendations often did not correspond to actual situational deficits on the ground — a policy-priority mismatch.</li>
<li><b>Overtaken by a Changing Landscape:</b> A 15-year gap before the next policy revision meant NHP 2002 increasingly lagged the changing socio-economic, technological, and epidemiological landscape it was meant to address.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">3. National Health Policy (NHP), 2017</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">NHP 2017, approved by the Union Cabinet in March 2017, is India's third National Health Policy, replacing NHP 2002. Its stated goal is to attain the highest possible level of health and well-being for all, through a preventive and promotive orientation across all developmental policies, and through universal access to good quality health care services without anyone facing financial hardship as a consequence.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;">It marks a shift from selective, disease-specific interventions towards <b>Universal Health Coverage (UHC)</b> as the organizing goal.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">3.2. Key Features</h3>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Primary &amp; Secondary/Tertiary Care</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>A larger, assured package of comprehensive primary health care delivered through <b>Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs)</b> — a shift from the earlier selective primary-care package to a comprehensive one, including geriatric, palliative, and rehabilitative care.</li>
<li>Free drugs, free diagnostics, and free emergency care services in all public hospitals, to provide financial protection at the secondary and tertiary levels.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Disease-Specific Elimination Targets</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>HIV/AIDS: achieve the global 90:90:90 target by 2020.</li>
<li>Eliminate Kala-Azar and Lymphatic Filariasis (in endemic pockets) by 2017.</li>
<li>Eliminate Leprosy by 2018.</li>
<li>Eliminate Tuberculosis by 2025.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Other Priorities</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Integration of AYUSH (traditional medicine) systems alongside modern treatment.</li>
<li>Prevention and management of Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) — diabetes, hypertension, cancer, and mental health conditions.</li>
<li>Digital health push — telemedicine, electronic health records, and e-governance, later operationalized as the National Digital Health Mission (NDHM).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Health Policy, 2017 approved by Cabinet"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">3.3. Ayushman Bharat — NHP 2017's Delivery Vehicle</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Launched in 2018, <b>Ayushman Bharat</b> is the flagship scheme operationalizing NHP 2017's UHC goal, combining preventive/primary care with financial protection at secondary/tertiary levels, through two pillars.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Components:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (AAMs)</b> — earlier called Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs); deliver Comprehensive Primary Health Care (CPHC) by expanding Reproductive &amp; Child Health and communicable-disease services to include NCD care (hypertension, diabetes, oral/breast/cervical cancer), mental health, ENT, ophthalmology, oral health, geriatric/palliative/trauma care, and wellness activities like yoga.</li>
<li><b>Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY)</b> — the world's largest publicly funded health assurance scheme, providing health cover of up to <b>₹5 lakh per family per year</b> for secondary and tertiary hospitalization. Cashless and paperless at point of care; no restriction on family size, age, or gender; all pre-existing conditions covered from day one; covers 3 days pre-hospitalization and 15 days post-hospitalization expenses.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Recent Expansion — Ayushman Vay Vandana Card</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Launched 29 October 2024: extends AB PM-JAY benefits to <b>all senior citizens aged 70 and above</b>, irrespective of socio-economic status — estimated to cover around 4.5 crore families (~6 crore individuals).</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB; Drishti IAS — "Ayushman Bharat @30"; Vision IAS.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">3.6. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Funding Shortfall:</b> Public health expenditure remains below the 2.5% of GDP target — the Union Government's own share (targeted at 1% of GDP) stood at just <b>0.29% of GDP in 2025-26</b>, down from 0.37% in 2020-21 — a gap unmet since NHP 2002 set the same kind of target for 2010.</li>
<li><b>High Out-of-Pocket Expenditure:</b> Low and declining central investment keeps households bearing a large share of healthcare costs directly, weakening preventive and primary care systems.</li>
<li><b>Workforce Shortage:</b> Significant deficit of doctors, nurses, and allied health professionals, concentrated particularly in rural regions.</li>
<li><b>Urban-Concentrated Infrastructure:</b> Healthcare infrastructure remains skewed towards urban areas, leaving rural populations underserved relative to the "golden hour" access goal.</li>
<li><b>Rising NCD Burden:</b> Non-communicable and emerging infectious diseases are increasing, adding pressure on a system still building out NCD care at the primary level.</li>
<li><b>Public-Private Coordination:</b> Coordination between public and private healthcare sectors remains limited despite NHP's intent to intermesh the two.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Meeting NHP 2017's financing targets, expanding the rural health workforce, and deepening public-private coordination remain central to converting Ayushman Bharat's coverage gains into genuine Universal Health Coverage.</p>
