<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Urban Housing &amp; Habitat Policy (NUHHP), 2007</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Seeks to promote various forms of public-private partnership to realize the goal of <b>"Affordable Housing for All"</b>, with special emphasis on the urban poor.</li>
<li>Recognizes the gap between housing demand and supply, and aims to give the poorest access to reasonably good housing — on rental and ownership bases — through suitable subsidization, since they cannot afford a house's full price.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Special emphasis on Scheduled Castes/Tribes/Backward Classes/Minorities and empowerment of women within the urban-poor category.</li>
<li>Housing and basic services for the urban poor integrated into the objectives of the <b>Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM)</b>.</li>
<li>Strategic tools: urban planning, increasing land supply, spatial incentives (additional Floor Area Ratio, Transferable Development Rights), increased fund flow, and effective solid-waste management.</li>
<li>Gives primacy to housing the urban poor <b>at or near their present location/workplace</b> — an in-situ slum rehabilitation approach.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Urban Housing &amp; Habitat Policy 2007".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle Scheme — Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban (PMAY-U)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Launched in 2015, PMAY-U is the primary vehicle operationalizing NUHHP 2007's "Affordable Housing for All" goal for EWS/LIG/MIG households, including slum dwellers.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Achievements:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Over <b>1.18 crore houses sanctioned</b> across 4,618 Urban Local Bodies; 1.14 crore grounded for construction; 83–88 lakh completed and delivered.</li>
<li><b>₹1,99,652 crore</b> committed, of which ₹1,63,926 crore released and ₹1,51,246 crore spent.</li>
<li>~<b>89 lakh houses</b> registered in a woman's name or joint ownership.</li>
<li>16 lakh houses built using new construction technologies; 917 crore person-days of construction employment generated.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban (PMAY-U)".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>PMAY-Urban 2.0 (2024)</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Cabinet-approved successor scheme complementing PMAY-U 1.0, continuing the affordable-housing push; the <b>Angikaar 2025</b> outreach campaign under it aims to bridge implementation and last-mile delivery gaps.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Cabinet approves Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban 2.0 Scheme"; Drishti IAS.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Land Availability:</b> non-availability of encumbrance-free land remains a major bottleneck for implementing agencies.</li>
<li><b>Beneficiary &amp; Clearance Delays:</b> unwillingness of some beneficiaries to relocate/participate, and delays in statutory clearances/NOCs, slow project completion.</li>
<li><b>Persistent Housing Backlog:</b> population growth, changing family patterns, and expanding slum/informal settlements keep the housing backlog and infrastructure deficit growing faster than delivery in some cities.</li>
<li><b>Sustainability Trade-offs:</b> balancing rapid housing expansion with environmental sustainability and climate resilience remains an open tension in scaling construction.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Cities Covered Under PMAY-U"; Drishti IAS — "Balancing Housing Expansion with Sustainability"; NITI Aayog — "India's Housing Conundrum".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Faster land-titling and clearance processes, deeper convergence with slum-upgradation and sustainability goals, and last-mile outreach campaigns like Angikaar are central to closing the remaining housing backlog under PMAY-U 2.0.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB; Drishti IAS; NITI Aayog.</p>
</div>
