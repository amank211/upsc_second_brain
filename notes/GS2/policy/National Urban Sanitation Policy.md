<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Urban Sanitation Policy (NUSP), 2008</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Envisions transforming all towns and cities of India into <b>100% sanitized, healthy, and livable</b> spaces, ensuring sustained public health and improved environmental outcomes.</li>
<li>Focuses on achievement of <b>outcomes</b> rather than mere construction of infrastructure, emphasizing capacity building at the city level.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Four Key Components</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Awareness Generation &amp; Behaviour Change</b> on sanitation's links to public and environmental health.</li>
<li><b>Open Defecation Free (ODF) Cities</b> — promoting access to safe household sanitation with disposal arrangements, plus community-operated toilets.</li>
<li><b>Integrated City-Wide Sanitation</b> — re-orienting institutions to prioritize sanitation in urban management.</li>
<li><b>Sanitary &amp; Safe Disposal</b> of 100% of human excreta and liquid waste from all sanitation facilities.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Urban Sanitation Policy".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;"><b>Special Focus:</b> hygienic and affordable sanitation facilities particularly for the urban poor and women.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle Schemes — Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban (SBM-U) &amp; AMRUT</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>SBM-Urban, 2014 — Phase 1 Achievements</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>All Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) declared <b>Open Defecation Free (ODF)</b>.</li>
<li><b>70%</b> of solid waste now scientifically processed.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>SBM-Urban 2.0 &amp; AMRUT 2.0 (launched 1 October 2021)</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Designed to make all cities <b>"Garbage Free"</b> and <b>"Water Secure"</b> over the mission period 2021-22 to 2025-26.</li>
<li>SBM-U 2.0 financial outlay: <b>₹1,41,600 crore</b> (central share ₹36,465 crore); AMRUT 2.0 total indicative outlay: <b>₹2,99,000 crore</b>.</li>
<li>Targets: statutory towns to reach at least <b>ODF+</b>, cities under 1 lakh population to reach <b>ODF++</b>, and all cities to achieve at least 3-star Garbage Free certification.</li>
<li>AMRUT has added <b>1.1 crore</b> household water-tap connections and <b>85 lakh</b> sewer connections so far.</li>
<li><b>Faecal Sludge and Septage Management (FSSM):</b> 700+ cities/towns in various implementation stages; 499 ULBs have already achieved ODF++ status.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "PM launches Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban 2.0 and AMRUT 2.0"; "ACHIEVEMENTS AND STATUS OF SBM-U"; Drishti IAS — "Faecal Sludge and Septage Management".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>ULB Capacity Gaps:</b> lack of funds, technical expertise, and appropriate institutional arrangements at the Urban Local Body level constrain waste-management rollout.</li>
<li><b>ULB Reluctance:</b> unwillingness among some ULBs to introduce proper collection, segregation, transportation, and treatment/disposal systems.</li>
<li><b>Citizen Apathy:</b> indifference towards waste management stemming from low awareness and weak community participation in hygiene practices.</li>
<li><b>Uneven FSSM Rollout:</b> faecal sludge and septage management is progressing, but coverage (700+ cities in progress, 499 at ODF++) still leaves many towns short of the policy's "100% safe disposal" goal.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: NITI Aayog — "Service &amp; Business Models: Faecal Sludge and Septage Management in Urban Areas"; Drishti IAS — "Making Swachh Bharat Mission A Reality".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Strengthening ULB technical capacity and financing, scaling up citizen behaviour-change campaigns, and leveraging platforms like the National Faecal Sludge and Septage Management (NFSSM) Alliance are central to converting ODF-status gains into fully "Garbage Free" and "Water Secure" cities under SBM-U 2.0.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: NITI Aayog; PIB; Drishti IAS.</p>
</div>
