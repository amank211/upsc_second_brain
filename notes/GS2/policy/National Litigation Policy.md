<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Litigation Policy (NLP), 2010</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Recognizes that the government and its agencies are the <b>predominant litigants</b> in Indian courts and tribunals; aims to transform government into an efficient, <b>"responsible litigant."</b></li>
<li>Aligned with the National Mission for Justice Delivery &amp; Legal Reforms goal of cutting average case pendency from <b>15 years to 3 years</b>.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Priority-Based Review:</b> pending cases with government as a party reviewed on priority, especially those touching welfare legislation, social reform, and weaker sections.</li>
<li><b>Monitoring &amp; Review Mechanism:</b> to sensitize government departments in important cases and avoid delay/neglect.</li>
<li><b>Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR):</b> encourages arbitration and other ADR mechanisms to reduce court burden.</li>
<li>Recognizes government's responsibility to protect citizens' rights, including fundamental rights, rather than litigate reflexively.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National Litigation Policy"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle — Legal Information Management &amp; Briefing System (LIMBS)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Launched by the Department of Legal Affairs in February 2016 — a single online platform monitoring the complete lifecycle of every court case where the Union of India is a party.</p>
<ul style="margin:8px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Currently tracks <b>7,23,123 live cases</b> across 53 Ministries/Departments, with 13,175 Ministry users and 18,458 empaneled advocates updating case status.</li>
<li>Includes a real-time <b>"Live Cases" Dashboard</b> for upcoming hearings, and an e-Bill module (integrated Feb 2025) for advocate payments via PFMS.</li>
<li>A fresh 2025 Law Ministry directive reinforces the push to <b>minimize Central Government litigation</b> outright.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Legal Information Management and Briefing System"; Vision IAS — "Ministry of Law Issues Directive to Minimize Litigation Involving Central Government".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Massive Pendency:</b> over 5 crore cases pending nationwide — ~63.3 lakh in High Courts, ~4.6 crore in district/subordinate courts.</li>
<li><b>Government Still the Biggest Litigant:</b> government agencies account for ~50% of litigation, with many cases being one department suing another — effectively outsourcing inter-departmental disputes to courts; poorly drafted orders have left tax revenue equal to 4.7% of GDP contested.</li>
<li><b>Judicial Capacity Shortfall:</b> India has just 21 judges per million people, against a recommended 50 per million; over 5,600 judicial vacancies exist, with High Courts at a 33% vacancy rate (2025).</li>
<li><b>Systemic Delay Drivers:</b> multiple appeals, frequent adjournments, frivolous litigation, vague laws, and weak case management compound pendency.</li>
<li><b>Underused ADR:</b> Alternative Dispute Resolution mechanisms remain under-deployed relative to rising case volumes; some cases have been pending over <b>50 years</b> (1,514 in High Courts, 1,390 in subordinate/district courts).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS — "Judicial Pendency"; Drishti IAS — "India's Judicial Pendency Crisis"; "Rising Judicial Pendency in India".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Enforcing NLP 2010's "responsible litigant" principle to cut down government-vs-government cases, filling judicial vacancies, and scaling ADR adoption are central to converting LIMBS's tracking capability into an actual reduction in pendency.</p>
