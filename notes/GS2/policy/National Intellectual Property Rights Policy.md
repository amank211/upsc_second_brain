<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) Policy, 2016</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Approved by Cabinet with the tagline <b>"Creative India; Innovative India"</b> — brings all forms of IP onto a single platform, recognizing inter-linkages between IP types, statutes, and agencies.</li>
<li>Covers <b>8 types</b> of intellectual property: patents, trademarks, industrial designs, copyrights, geographical indications, semiconductor IC layout designs, trade secrets, and plant varieties.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Seven Objectives</b>
<ol style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>IPR Awareness — of the economic, social, and cultural benefits of IPRs.</li>
<li>Generation of IPRs.</li>
<li>Legal and Legislative Framework — strong IPR laws balancing rights-holders with public interest.</li>
<li>Administration and Management — modernized, service-oriented IPR administration.</li>
<li>Commercialization of IPRs — realizing value from IP assets.</li>
<li>Enforcement and Adjudication — strengthening mechanisms against IPR infringement.</li>
<li>Human Capital Development — expanding teaching, training, research, and skilling capacity in IPRs.</li>
</ol>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "National IPR Policy"; "Cabinet approves National Intellectual Property Rights Policy".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle — Cell for IPR Promotion and Management (CIPAM)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Established under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) to implement NIPR Policy 2016 — building public awareness, facilitating IPR filing, providing a commercialization platform, and coordinating implementation across ministries/stakeholders.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Scheme for IPR Awareness — "Creative India; Innovative India":</b> targets 4,000+ awareness workshops/seminars for students, youth, authors, artists, MSMEs, and startups.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Achievements:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's Global Innovation Index rank improved from <b>81st (2015)</b> to <b>52nd (2019)</b> to <b>39th (2024, out of 133 economies)</b>.</li>
<li>India now ranks <b>6th globally</b> in patent applications, with the fastest growth among top-20 origins in 2023 — a fifth consecutive year of double-digit growth; also placed in the <b>top 10</b> globally for patents, trademarks, and industrial designs (WIPO 2024).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "India secures position in top 10 countries in Patents, Trademarks, and Industrial Designs: WIPO 2024 Report".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Slow Patent Processing:</b> average patent approval takes ~58 months in India, versus ~20 months in China and ~21 months in the US.</li>
<li><b>Patent Growth Stagnation:</b> after years of double-digit growth, patent applications stagnated in 2024 with few approvals, pointing to underlying low private-sector R&amp;D.</li>
<li><b>Low R&amp;D Investment:</b> India spends just 0.65% of GDP on R&amp;D, versus China (2.43%), South Korea (2.5%), and Brazil (1.15%).</li>
<li><b>Foreign-Entity Dominance:</b> over 74% of patents granted in India go to foreign entities, reflecting weak domestic IP generation relative to NIPR's own "generation" objective.</li>
<li><b>Enforcement Gaps:</b> piracy and counterfeiting remain significant despite enforcement efforts; abolition of the Intellectual Property Appellate Board (IPAB) left a void in handling IP appeals.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS — "Stagnation of India's Patent Growth"; "Revolutionizing India's Patent Ecosystem"; "WIPO World Intellectual Property Indicators 2024 Report".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Speeding up patent examination, raising private R&amp;D investment, strengthening domestic (not just foreign) IP generation, and restoring a dedicated IP appellate mechanism are central to converting India's GII/WIPO ranking gains into a genuinely self-sustaining innovation ecosystem.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Drishti IAS; PIB.</p>
</div>
