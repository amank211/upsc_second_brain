<h2 style="background-color:#2c3e50; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. National Policy on Biofuels (NPB), 2018 (amended 2022)</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef4f3; border:1px solid #bcd4cf; border-left:5px solid #2c7a6b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Overview</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Aims to reduce import dependence via fuel blending, enhance energy security, save foreign exchange, address environmental concerns, and boost domestic agriculture.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border:1px solid #e0c6a3; border-left:5px solid #c0392b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Key Features</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>The 2022 amendment advanced the target of <b>20% ethanol blending in petrol (E20)</b> to Ethanol Supply Year 2025-26, from the original 2030 deadline; a <b>30% blending target by 2030</b> is now envisaged.</li>
<li>Emphasizes second-generation (2G) biofuels from forest/agricultural residues, and diversified feedstock — corn, cassava, rotten potatoes, damaged food grains, sugarcane juice/molasses, rice straw, cotton stalk, bagasse, etc.</li>
<li>Coordinated by the <b>National Biofuel Coordination Committee</b>.</li>
<li>Support measures: administered price mechanism for ethanol procurement, GST cut to 5% on ethanol for the EBP Programme, amendment of the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act to ease inter/intra-state ethanol movement, and simplified procurement by Oil Marketing Companies.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Cabinet approves Amendments to the National Policy on Biofuels-2018"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Biofuels in India".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.1. Vehicle — Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<p style="margin:0;">Originally launched for 5% ethanol blending in 9 States and 4 UTs (also featured in the Auto Fuel Policy, 2003); now operates under NPB 2018, driven by the Ministry of Petroleum &amp; Natural Gas.</p>
<p style="margin:10px 0 0 0;"><b>Achievements:</b></p>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Ethanol production rose from <b>38 crore litres (2014)</b> to over <b>660 crore litres (2025)</b>.</li>
<li>Blending rose from <b>1.5% (2014)</b> to <b>20% (2025)</b> — E20 hit 5 years ahead of the original 2030 target.</li>
<li>Ethanol procurement has earned farmers <b>₹1.18 lakh crore</b> and distilleries <b>₹1.96 lakh crore</b>, boosting rural employment in UP, Maharashtra, and Bihar.</li>
<li><b>₹1.36 lakh crore</b> saved in forex from reduced crude oil imports.</li>
<li>As of April 2026, all petrol sold nationwide must be E20 (min. RON 95); E20 cuts carbon emissions by ~30% versus E10.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: PIB — "Ethanol Blended Petrol Programme- Q&amp;A"; Drishti IAS — "India Achieves 20% Ethanol Blending in Petrol".</p>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#4a6b7c; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:24px;">1.2. Key Challenges</h3>

<div style="background-color:#fbe9e7; border:1px solid #e6b0aa; border-left:5px solid #a93226; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<ul style="margin:0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Heavy Water Footprint:</b> producing 1 litre of sugarcane-based ethanol needs ~3,630 litres of water; rice-based ethanol needs ~10,790 litres — the life-cycle water footprint ranges 230–7,150 litres per litre of ethanol depending on feedstock/technology.</li>
<li><b>Groundwater Depletion:</b> intensive sugarcane/paddy cultivation for ethanol has worsened groundwater depletion in Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh; sugarcane and paddy together already consume 70% of India's irrigation water (NITI Aayog).</li>
<li><b>Food-Fuel Conflict:</b> diverting food-grade crops to ethanol raises food-security concerns, an issue that sharpens in poor-monsoon years.</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS — "India's ethanol policy must count water, sustainability costs"; Vajiram &amp; Ravi — "Ethanol Blending in India, Benefits, Challenges, Concerns".</p>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#eaf7ec; border:1px solid #a8d5b0; border-left:5px solid #388e3c; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-top:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Way Forward</b>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Scaling second-generation biofuels from agricultural residues (paddy straw, etc.) instead of food/water-intensive crops is central to sustaining the 20%+ blending trajectory without deepening groundwater depletion or food-security risk.</p>
<p style="margin:12px 0 0 0; font-size:0.78em; color:#7a6a55; font-style:italic;">Source: Vision IAS; Vajiram &amp; Ravi; PIB.</p>
</div>
