Comprehensive reference on the United Nations system — principal organs, specialized agencies, funds & programmes, and related bodies — with structure, mandate, and UPSC angle for each. #GS2

<h1 style="background-color:#1a3a5c; color:#ffffff; padding:14px 20px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1.15em; letter-spacing:0.5px; text-align:center;">THE UNITED NATIONS & ITS SYSTEM</h1>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">1. The UN System: Overview</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:12px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>What is the UN Family?</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>The United Nations is a 193-member intergovernmental organisation founded in 1945 to maintain international peace, security, and cooperation — successor to the failed League of Nations.</li>
<li>The broader "UN system" or "UN family" includes: 6 principal organs + 15 specialized agencies (treaty-linked, legally independent) + funds &amp; programmes (UNGA subsidiary bodies) + related organisations (e.g. IAEA, WTO).</li>
<li>HQ: New York; additional offices in Geneva, Vienna, Nairobi. Official languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish.</li>
<li>Charter signed at the San Francisco Conference, 26 June 1945; entered into force 24 October 1945 (UN Day). India was a founding signatory.</li>
<li>Budget: two-track — regular budget (~$3.2 bn/year, assessed contributions) and peacekeeping budget (~$6.3 bn/year). US contributes ~22%, China ~15.2%, Japan ~8.0% of the regular budget.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:24px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Direct syllabus entry:</b> "Important International institutions, agencies and fora — structure, mandate" (GS2 Mains).</li>
<li>Questions asked as: "Describe the structure and functions of X", "Critically examine India's role in X", "How does X relate to India's foreign policy?"</li>
<li>India is a founding member, largest peacekeeping troop contributor, and seeks a permanent UNSC seat — making India-UN relations a recurring theme.</li>
<li>Knowing which UN body is relevant to a current affair (e.g. WFP = food crises, UNHCR = refugee crises) helps write context-rich answers.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">2. The Six Principal Organs</h2>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.1 UN General Assembly (UNGA)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>All 193 UN member states — each has one vote regardless of size or GDP (sovereign equality principle).</li>
<li>Main deliberative, policymaking, and representative body. Meets in regular sessions (September–December) and special/emergency sessions.</li>
<li>Adopts resolutions on any matter within UN scope — but resolutions are <b>non-binding</b> (except on budgetary/internal matters).</li>
<li>Key powers: approves the UN budget, elects non-permanent UNSC members, elects ICJ judges (jointly with UNSC), admits new members, appoints the Secretary-General on UNSC recommendation.</li>
<li>6 main committees: Disarmament (C1), Economic/Financial (C2), Social/Humanitarian/Cultural (C3), Special Political/Decolonisation (C4), Administrative/Budgetary (C5), Legal (C6).</li>
<li>"Uniting for Peace" Resolution 377 (1950): if UNSC is deadlocked, UNGA can convene an Emergency Special Session and recommend collective measures.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India often uses UNGA as a platform for multilateral diplomacy — PM's addresses at UNGA High-Level General Debate are significant foreign policy statements.</li>
<li>India co-sponsored UNGA Resolution on International Day of Yoga (adopted 2014) and International Solar Alliance.</li>
<li>Uniting for Peace mechanism: relevant when UNSC is paralysed (e.g. on Ukraine, Gaza) — UNGA stepped in with emergency special sessions.</li>
<li>UNGA resolutions on Palestine, Ukraine ceasefire: India's abstentions are frequent UPSC/essay themes on India's "strategic autonomy".</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.2 UN Security Council (UNSC)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>15 members:</b> 5 permanent (P5 — US, UK, France, Russia, China) with veto power + 10 non-permanent elected by UNGA for 2-year terms (5 replaced each year), from regional groupings.</li>
<li>Only UN organ with <b>binding authority</b> — resolutions under Chapter VII (threats to peace, acts of aggression) are legally binding on all member states.</li>
<li>Powers: authorise peacekeeping missions, impose sanctions, refer cases to ICC, recommend Secretary-General to UNGA, recommend new member admission.</li>
<li>Presidency rotates monthly among the 15 members alphabetically.</li>
<li>Veto: any P5 member's single "No" vote defeats a substantive resolution — even if the other 14 vote in favour. An abstention by a P5 member does NOT constitute a veto.</li>
<li>P5 veto usage (major): US has vetoed ~80+ times (many on Israel/Palestine); Russia ~120+ times; China's use increased since 1970s. Since 2011: Russia vetoed ~19 resolutions (Syria, Ukraine); US vetoed Gaza ceasefire resolutions (2023–24).</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India has been a non-permanent member 8 times (most recently 2021–22, as part of Asia-Pacific group).</li>
<li>India's permanent membership bid: backed by US, France, Russia, UK — China remains the key obstacle (also blocks Japan and Germany).</li>
<li>UNSC reform is a very high-frequency UPSC topic — know the G4 model, Uniting for Consensus, African bloc (C10) positions (see Section 6).</li>
<li>Veto paralysis on Ukraine and Gaza has renewed urgency for reform; "Veto Initiative" (Liechtenstein-led) passed UNGA in 2022: any P5 veto now triggers automatic UNGA debate.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.3 Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>54 member states elected by UNGA for 3-year terms; 18 replaced each year. Presidency rotates annually.</li>
<li>Coordinates work of 15 specialized agencies, 10 functional commissions (e.g. Commission on the Status of Women — CSW, Commission on Narcotic Drugs — CND), 5 regional commissions (ESCAP, ECA, ECE, ECLAC, ESCWA).</li>
<li>High-Level Political Forum on Sustainable Development (HLPF): the main UN platform for SDG follow-up and review, convened under ECOSOC auspices each July.</li>
<li>Accreditation of NGOs to the UN system goes through ECOSOC.</li>
<li>Not a legislative body — resolutions are recommendations; real influence is through coordination and norm-setting.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India regularly serves on ECOSOC; has been elected to CSW — important for gender-related questions.</li>
<li>ESCAP (Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific): India is a member — relevant to Asia-Pacific regional cooperation questions.</li>
<li>SDG Voluntary National Reviews (VNRs) are presented at HLPF — India has submitted VNRs (2017, 2020, 2023); useful for SDG-related essay/GS answers.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.4 International Court of Justice (ICJ)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>15 judges elected for 9-year terms (5 replaced every 3 years) by both UNGA and UNSC simultaneously; HQ: Peace Palace, The Hague, Netherlands.</li>
<li>Two jurisdictions: (a) <b>Contentious cases</b> — binding judgments on disputes between states that consent to jurisdiction; (b) <b>Advisory opinions</b> — non-binding legal opinions requested by UN organs or agencies.</li>
<li>States must consent to ICJ jurisdiction (through treaty clauses, optional clause declarations, or special agreement — "compromis").</li>
<li>ICJ cannot try individuals — that is the International Criminal Court (ICC), a separate body (see Section 5).</li>
<li>Judgments are final and binding on parties, but ICJ has no enforcement mechanism — relies on UNSC (which is itself subject to P5 veto).</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Kulbhushan Jadhav Case (India v Pakistan, 2019):</b> ICJ ordered Pakistan to review Jadhav's death sentence and ensure consular access — landmark use of the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations. India won on key points; very high UPSC frequency.</li>
<li>India has accepted ICJ's compulsory jurisdiction with reservations (excluding disputes with Commonwealth nations and those arising before 1974).</li>
<li>Advisory opinions relevant to UPSC: Legal consequences of building a wall in Occupied Palestinian Territory (2004); Nuclear Weapons advisory opinion (1996).</li>
<li>Distinguish ICJ (inter-state, part of UN) from ICC (criminal court for individuals, not a UN organ) — a common confusion in UPSC answers.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.5 The Secretariat</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>The UN's civil service: headed by the <b>Secretary-General (SG)</b> — the chief administrative officer, appointed by UNGA on UNSC recommendation for a 5-year renewable term.</li>
<li>Current SG: <b>António Guterres</b> (Portugal) — 9th SG, since Jan 2017, re-appointed for a second term (2022–2026).</li>
<li>The SG can bring to UNSC's attention any matter threatening international peace and security (Article 99 of the Charter) — a rarely used but politically significant power.</li>
<li>Heads departments covering peacekeeping (DPPA, DPO), humanitarian affairs (OCHA), economic/social affairs (DESA), legal affairs, safety/security, management strategy, etc.</li>
<li>~44,000 staff internationally from 190+ countries; staff are international civil servants — owe loyalty to the UN, not to any government.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Past SGs and their contributions are frequently asked: Trygve Lie (1st, Norway), Dag Hammarskjöld (died in plane crash 1961, Congo crisis), U Thant (1st Asian SG, Myanmar), Kurt Waldheim, Javier Pérez de Cuéllar, Boutros Boutros-Ghali (Africa's 1st), Kofi Annan (Nobel Peace Prize 2001, Ghana), Ban Ki-moon (South Korea), Guterres.</li>
<li>India has never held the SG position — but senior Indian diplomats have held key Secretariat roles (e.g. Shashi Tharoor as Under-Secretary-General for Communications).</li>
<li>Guterres's UN80 reform initiative (2025) — restructuring the Secretariat to cut bureaucracy and redirect funds to operations — is a current affairs angle.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">2.6 Trusteeship Council</h3>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:10px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Structure &amp; Mandate</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>Established to oversee decolonisation of Non-Self-Governing territories placed under UN trusteeship after WWII; composed of P5 members.</li>
<li>Administered 11 trust territories; last territory — Palau — became independent in 1994. Council <b>suspended operations 1 November 1994</b>; technically still exists in the UN Charter but meets only on an extraordinary basis.</li>
<li>Role is now historical — decolonisation effectively complete within its mandate.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:24px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's own decolonisation of Goa (1961) was debated in the UNSC (not Trusteeship Council) — US, UK, France supported Portugal; USSR vetoed the resolution condemning India.</li>
<li>Some reform proposals suggest repurposing the Trusteeship Council for "global commons" governance (deep seabed, outer space, Antarctica) — an emerging UPSC conceptual question.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">3. UN Specialized Agencies</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 16px; margin-bottom:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Nature:</b> Independent intergovernmental organisations linked to the UN through negotiated agreements with ECOSOC. They have their own constitutions, governing bodies, budgets, and membership (which may differ from the UN's 193). They report to ECOSOC and UNGA but are <b>not subordinate</b> to them.
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.1 World Health Organization (WHO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Geneva | <b>Founded:</b> 1948 | <b>Members:</b> 194 | <b>DG:</b> Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus (Ethiopia, 2017–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Directing and coordinating authority for international health; sets norms/standards (IHR — International Health Regulations); leads global disease surveillance, vaccination programmes, and health emergency response.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key reports/instruments:</b> World Health Statistics, Global Burden of Disease, IHR (2005), Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's role in COVID-19 vaccine equity: "Vaccine Maitri" initiative; debate over WHO's response timeliness — UPSC essay/GS2 theme.</li>
<li>India vs WHO on tobacco regulations and plain packaging — India opposed certain FCTC guidelines; relevant to health policy questions.</li>
<li>WHO's NCD (Non-Communicable Disease) targets and India's burden of NCDs: diabetes, hypertension, cancer — linkage to National Health Policy 2017.</li>
<li>US under Trump (both tenures) threatened to withdraw from WHO — implications for global health governance.</li>
<li>Pandemic Treaty negotiations (2023–25): India's position on TRIPS waiver and technology transfer for pandemic preparedness.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.2 Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Rome | <b>Founded:</b> 1945 | <b>Members:</b> 195 | <b>DG:</b> Qu Dongyu (China, 2019–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Achieve food security, eliminate hunger, improve nutrition, and ensure sustainable management of natural resources. Publishes the annual <b>State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World (SOFI)</b> report.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key programmes:</b> Codex Alimentarius (food safety standards), International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources, Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems (GIAHS), Desert Locust monitoring.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>SOFI report data on global hunger and India's ranking — used in food security questions; India has the largest number of undernourished people globally per recent SOFI reports.</li>
<li>India's 2023 GIAHS designations (e.g., Kuttanad below sea-level farming, Koraput traditional agriculture) — culture and heritage angle.</li>
<li>FAO's Desert Locust early warning system: India faced locust attacks (2019–20) in Rajasthan — FAO coordination angle.</li>
<li>India's push for Millets/Shree Anna: India proposed and co-led the 2023 International Year of Millets at the UN/FAO — PM Modi's initiative, major 2023 UPSC current affairs.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.3 UNESCO (UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Paris | <b>Founded:</b> 1946 | <b>Members:</b> 194 | <b>DG:</b> Audrey Azoulay (France, 2017–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Build peace through education, science, and culture; promotes free flow of information, cultural heritage protection, and press freedom. Administers the World Heritage Convention (1972).</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key instruments/lists:</b> World Heritage List (cultural/natural sites), Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) List, Memory of the World Register, Global Education Monitoring (GEM) Report.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India has <b>42 UNESCO World Heritage Sites</b> (as of 2024) — Cultural, Natural, and Mixed; list changes frequently — always check latest for Prelims.</li>
<li>India's ICH elements inscribed: Yoga, Kumbh Mela, Garba, Durga Puja, Vedic Chanting, Chhau dance — high Prelims frequency.</li>
<li>US and Israel withdrew from UNESCO (2018, over anti-Israel bias); the US rejoined in 2023 under Biden. India has remained a consistent member.</li>
<li>India (like the US earlier) had withdrawn from UNESCO during 2003–2006 — common misconception; India actually rejoined in 2006 after being inactive. Check current status carefully.</li>
<li>GEM Report: tracks SDG-4 (Quality Education) — India's literacy, dropout, and learning outcome data cited here.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.4 International Labour Organization (ILO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Geneva | <b>Founded:</b> 1919 (pre-dates the UN) | <b>Members:</b> 187 | <b>DG:</b> Gilbert Houngbo (Togo, 2022–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Set international labour standards, promote rights at work, encourage decent employment opportunities, enhance social protection. Unique tripartite structure: governments + employers + workers all vote.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key instruments:</b> 190 Conventions (legally binding treaties on labour rights); 8 fundamental/core conventions cover child labour, forced labour, discrimination, freedom of association. World Employment and Social Outlook (WESO) report. Decent Work Agenda.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India has ratified only <b>47 of 190 ILO Conventions</b>, including all 8 fundamental conventions (after ratifying the last two — Convention 87 on freedom of association and Convention 98 on collective bargaining — in 2021). This was a major reform step.</li>
<li>India's 4 Labour Codes (2019–2020) were partly motivated by desire to align domestic law with ILO standards — UPSC GS2/GS3 overlap.</li>
<li>ILO's child labour data and India's PENCIL portal (for child labour elimination) — GS2 social justice angle.</li>
<li>WESO Report: global unemployment and informal employment data — useful for prelims and essay on "decent work".</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.5 International Monetary Fund (IMF)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Washington D.C. | <b>Founded:</b> 1944 (Bretton Woods) | <b>Members:</b> 190 | <b>MD:</b> Kristalina Georgieva (Bulgaria, 2019–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Promote international monetary cooperation, exchange rate stability, balanced trade, and economic growth; provides temporary financial assistance to countries facing balance-of-payments problems.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key instruments:</b> Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) — reserve asset; Article IV Consultations — annual economic health checks; World Economic Outlook (WEO) report; Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's IMF quota share is ~2.75% (16th largest) — underrepresentation given India's ~7% share of global GDP; quota reform is a recurring demand.</li>
<li>India received IMF assistance in 1991 (BOP crisis, pledged gold reserves) — pivotal historical moment for economic liberalisation; and in 1981. India has not borrowed from IMF since 1993.</li>
<li>WEO growth forecasts for India are cited frequently in current affairs — IMF often projects India as fastest-growing major economy.</li>
<li>SDR basket composition (USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, GBP) — India pushing to include INR eventually.</li>
<li>IMF conditionalities (structural adjustment) and their social costs — relevant to comparative development economics questions.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.6 World Bank Group</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Washington D.C. | <b>Founded:</b> 1944 (Bretton Woods) | <b>President:</b> Ajay Banga (India-origin, US, 2023–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Five institutions:</b> IBRD (loans to middle-income countries) | IDA (concessional credit/grants to poorest countries) | IFC (private sector investment in developing countries) | MIGA (political risk insurance) | ICSID (investor-state dispute settlement).</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key reports:</b> World Development Report (WDR), Doing Business Report (now discontinued), Poverty and Shared Prosperity Report, Human Capital Index.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India is the <b>largest borrower</b> from IBRD historically and regularly among the top IDA borrowers; significant financing for infrastructure, education, health, agriculture.</li>
<li>Ajay Banga's appointment as World Bank president (2023) — first person of Indian origin; signal of India's growing global economic stature.</li>
<li>Human Capital Index ranks India — relevant to human development questions.</li>
<li>World Bank's Ease of Doing Business index: India rose from 142 (2014) to 63 (2019) — "ease of doing business" reform narrative (the index was discontinued in 2021 due to data integrity concerns).</li>
<li>World Bank–India collaboration on smart cities, Namami Gange, PMGSY road projects — GS3 linkage.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.7 International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Montreal | <b>Founded:</b> 1944 (Chicago Convention) | <b>Members:</b> 193 | <b>SG:</b> Juan Carlos Salazar (Colombia, 2021–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Sets standards and regulations for international civil aviation safety, efficiency, security, and environmental sustainability (CORSIA — Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's aviation sector is one of the fastest-growing globally; UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) scheme expands regional connectivity — ICAO's safety/standards framework governs Indian airports.</li>
<li>CORSIA (ICAO's carbon offsetting scheme for aviation) and India's participation — relevant to climate and aviation policy questions.</li>
<li>ICAO's audit of India's aviation safety oversight — India achieved good ratings, relevant to "Make in India" in aerospace.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.8 International Maritime Organization (IMO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> London | <b>Founded:</b> 1948 | <b>Members:</b> 175 | <b>SG:</b> Arsenio Dominguez (Panama, 2024–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Safety and security of international shipping; prevention of marine pollution by ships (MARPOL Convention); sets standards for seafarer training (STCW Convention). Adopted 2050 net-zero GHG target for shipping sector (revised 2023).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India has the world's largest ship-recycling industry (Alang, Gujarat) — subject to IMO's Hong Kong Convention on safe ship recycling.</li>
<li>India's blue economy and Sagarmala project — IMO standards govern port development and maritime transport.</li>
<li>Djibouti Code of Conduct (on piracy in Indian Ocean) — India a key player; links to India's broader Indo-Pacific/SAGAR ("Security and Growth for All in the Region") vision.</li>
<li>IMO's 2050 net-zero target for shipping: India's position on "common but differentiated responsibilities" in maritime decarbonisation.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.9 World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Geneva | <b>Founded:</b> 1967 | <b>Members:</b> 193 | <b>DG:</b> Daren Tang (Singapore, 2020–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Promote protection of intellectual property worldwide through treaties (Paris Convention, Berne Convention, Patent Cooperation Treaty — PCT, Madrid System for trademarks). Global Innovation Index (GII) published annually.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's <b>Geographical Indications (GI) tags</b> — registered under WIPO's Lisbon Agreement framework; Darjeeling Tea, Basmati, Kanjeevaram Silk, etc. GI tag disputes (Basmati with Pakistan) are high-frequency Prelims topics.</li>
<li><b>Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)</b>: India's initiative to document traditional knowledge in WIPO databases to prevent bio-piracy — landmark defensive IP strategy; frequently asked in GS2/GS3.</li>
<li>Global Innovation Index: India's rank improving (2023: 40th) — used in "India as innovation hub" narratives.</li>
<li>TRIPS Agreement and WIPO: India's position on compulsory licensing for medicines — Section 84 of the Indian Patents Act used for Sorafenib (Bayer case, 2012) — balancing IP protection with public health access.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">3.10 Other Specialized Agencies (Summary)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>ITU (International Telecommunication Union)</b> — Geneva, 1865/1947; oldest intergovernmental org; sets global telecom and radio spectrum standards. India angle: Digital India, 5G spectrum auctions, ITU's Digital Inclusion Index, India's ITU Council membership.</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>WMO (World Meteorological Organization)</b> — Geneva, 1950; coordinates global weather observation, climate science, early warning systems. India angle: India Meteorological Department (IMD), Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI — India-led), climate data for NDCs.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>IFAD (International Fund for Agricultural Development)</b> — Rome, 1977; provides low-interest loans and grants to developing countries for agriculture and rural development. India angle: India is both a donor and recipient; funds projects in tribal/rural India.</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UNIDO (UN Industrial Development Organization)</b> — Vienna, 1966/1985; promotes industrial development in developing countries. India angle: Make in India, industrial capacity building, green industrial transitions. (Note: the US and UK both left UNIDO, questioning its effectiveness.)</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UNWTO (UN World Tourism Organization)</b> — Madrid, 2003; promotes responsible and sustainable tourism. India angle: "Incredible India" campaign, India's tourism receipts and rankings, Yoga tourism.</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:20px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UPU (Universal Postal Union)</b> — Bern, 1874; one of the oldest international organisations; regulates international postal services. India angle: India Post's role in financial inclusion (India Post Payments Bank) — though UPU's primary concern is physical mail standards.</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">4. UN Funds and Programmes</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 16px; margin-bottom:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Nature:</b> Subsidiary bodies of UNGA — not independent treaty organisations. Funded primarily by voluntary contributions from member states. Report to UNGA and ECOSOC. Headed by an Executive Director (not a Director-General). Their work is often more operational/on-the-ground than the specialized agencies.
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.1 UNDP (UN Development Programme)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> New York | <b>Founded:</b> 1965 | <b>Administrator:</b> Achim Steiner (Germany, 2017–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Largest UN development network; supports countries to end poverty and achieve sustainable development. Publishes the annual <b>Human Development Report (HDR)</b> with the Human Development Index (HDI), Gender Inequality Index (GII), and Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI — jointly with OPHI, Oxford).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's <b>HDI rank: 134/193</b> (HDR 2023/24, Medium Human Development category) — used in every human development GS2 answer.</li>
<li>MPI: India's 2023 national MPI shows significant poverty decline — 415 million people lifted out of multidimensional poverty in 15 years (2005/06 to 2019/21) — major data point.</li>
<li>GII (Gender Inequality Index): India ranks 108/166 (2022) — gender question answers.</li>
<li>UNDP administers the Global Environment Facility (GEF) — links to India's climate finance.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.2 UNICEF (UN Children's Fund)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> New York | <b>Founded:</b> 1946 | <b>ED:</b> Catherine Russell (USA, 2022–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Promotes the survival, protection, and development of children globally; implements the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC, 1989). Publishes State of the World's Children report.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India has ratified the CRC — child rights framework underpins the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, JJ Act, etc.</li>
<li>UNICEF's POSHAN (nutrition) data on India: stunting, wasting, anaemia rates — cited in child nutrition policy questions.</li>
<li>UNICEF's role in India's immunisation (Universal Immunisation Programme) — Mission Indradhanush linkage.</li>
<li>Child labour, child marriage, adolescent education — all UNICEF focus areas that intersect with India's welfare schemes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.3 UNFPA (UN Population Fund)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> New York | <b>Founded:</b> 1969 | <b>ED:</b> Dr. Natalia Kanem (Panama, 2017–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Supports reproductive health, family planning, maternal health, and gender equality; leads global population data and publishes the State of World Population report. Tracks demographic data including global population milestones.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>UNFPA declared India the world's most populous country (surpassing China) in April 2023 — 1.429 billion — a major current affairs data point.</li>
<li>India's demographic dividend window (2005–2055 approx.) — UNFPA's population projections underpin skill development, education, and employment policy discourse.</li>
<li>National Population Policy 2000 and India's family planning programme — UNFPA technical partnership.</li>
<li>Sex-ratio and missing women issues — UNFPA data supports Beti Bachao Beti Padhao-type schemes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.4 UNEP (UN Environment Programme)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Nairobi (only major UN body HQed in Global South) | <b>Founded:</b> 1972 (Stockholm Conference) | <b>ED:</b> Inger Andersen (Denmark, 2019–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> The leading global environmental authority; sets global environmental agenda, promotes sustainable development, catalyses environmental action. Convenes the UN Environment Assembly (UNEA) — highest-level global decision-making body on the environment. Secretariat for key treaties: CITES, Montreal Protocol, CBD, Basel/Rotterdam/Stockholm Conventions.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India's NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) — submitted under the Paris Agreement (UNFCCC — a related but separate convention secretariat hosted by UNEP). India's updated NDCs (2022): 50% non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030, 45% emissions intensity reduction vs 2005.</li>
<li>India's <b>LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment)</b> Mission — launched at COP26 (2021) by PM Modi, subsequently endorsed at UNEA — India's signature global environmental initiative.</li>
<li>UNEA resolution on plastics pollution treaty (2022): India co-sponsored — global plastics treaty negotiations (INC process).</li>
<li>Emissions Gap Report (UNEP): tracks whether countries' pledges are sufficient to meet 1.5°C/2°C — data used in climate change answers.</li>
<li>CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species): India's tiger, elephant, rhino, lion-tailed macaque — CITES Appendix I protection. Project Tiger, Project Elephant — domestic linkage.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.5 UNHCR (UN High Commissioner for Refugees)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Geneva | <b>Founded:</b> 1950 | <b>HC:</b> Filippo Grandi (Italy, 2016–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Protects and supports refugees, asylum seekers, internally displaced persons (IDPs), stateless persons, and returnees. Administers the 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol. UNHCR won the Nobel Peace Prize twice (1954, 1981).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle — CRITICAL</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>India is NOT a signatory to the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol</b> — the most important India-UNHCR fact. India has no domestic refugee law; handles refugees on an ad hoc basis through the Foreigners Act and administrative discretion.</li>
<li>Despite this, India hosts significant refugee populations: Tibetans (~100,000), Sri Lankan Tamils, Rohingya (~40,000), Afghan refugees — all managed without a legal framework.</li>
<li>UNHCR operates in India with limited formal mandate; India-UNHCR relations are complex — India views refugees through a security and sovereignty lens.</li>
<li>Global Compact on Refugees (GCR, 2018): India did not formally endorse it. Contrast with India's historical tradition of hosting refugees (Tibetans, Bangladeshis, etc.).</li>
<li>"Non-refoulement" principle (core of refugee law): India is not bound by it legally but often observes it in practice — key conceptual distinction for essays.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.6 WFP (World Food Programme)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Rome | <b>Founded:</b> 1961 | <b>ED:</b> Cindy McCain (USA, 2023–) | <b>Nobel Peace Prize: 2020</b></div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> World's largest humanitarian organisation addressing hunger and promoting food security; delivers food assistance in emergencies and works with communities to improve nutrition and build resilience. Governed jointly by UN and FAO.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>WFP awarded Nobel Peace Prize 2020 for efforts to combat hunger and its role in bettering conditions for peace — quote-worthy in essay introductions.</li>
<li>India's food security architecture (PDS, NFSA 2013) vs WFP's public stockholding programme: WFP actually sources food from India for its operations globally — India is a supplier, not a major recipient.</li>
<li>India's WTO dispute with developed countries over public stockholding of foodgrains for food security (peace clause) — WFP's mandate is directly relevant here.</li>
<li>Ukraine war's impact on global food supply (wheat, sunflower oil) — WFP's funding crises in 2022-24 are current affairs context.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">4.7 Other Funds &amp; Programmes (Summary)</h3>

<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UNODC (UN Office on Drugs and Crime)</b> — Vienna, 1997; combats illicit drugs, crime, terrorism. India angle: NDPS Act, Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB), India's role in combating drug trafficking from the "Golden Crescent" (Afghanistan-Pakistan-Iran) and "Golden Triangle" (Myanmar-Laos-Thailand).</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UN Women</b> — New York, 2010 (merged INSTRAW, OSAGI, DAW, UNIFEM); works for gender equality and women's empowerment. India angle: tracks SDG-5 progress, partners on Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, women's political participation, ending violence against women.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:20px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UNCTAD (UN Conference on Trade and Development)</b> — Geneva, 1964; promotes developing countries' integration into the world economy; publishes Trade and Development Report, World Investment Report (FDI data). India angle: FDI inflows data, investor protection, preferential trade agreements; India's "south-south trade" advocacy.</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">5. Related Bodies</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 16px; margin-bottom:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Nature:</b> These organisations are not formal UN specialized agencies but are closely related to the UN system through relationship agreements or overlapping mandates. Some (IAEA) have a reporting relationship with both UNGA and UNSC; others (WTO, ICC) are fully independent but operationally intertwined with the UN.
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">5.1 IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Vienna | <b>Founded:</b> 1957 | <b>Members:</b> 178 | <b>DG:</b> Rafael Mariano Grossi (Argentina, 2019–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> The world's "Atoms for Peace and Development" organisation — promotes peaceful uses of nuclear energy while ensuring nuclear technology is not used for military purposes. Conducts safeguards inspections; reports to both UNGA and UNSC (uniquely). Won Nobel Peace Prize 2005.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle — HIGH PRIORITY</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>India is NOT an NPT signatory</b> but has safeguards agreements with IAEA covering its civilian nuclear facilities (post 2008 India-US Civil Nuclear Deal / 123 Agreement).</li>
<li>India-specific safeguards agreement (INFCIRC/754) — India-IAEA Additional Protocol: India's civilian reactors under IAEA safeguards; military reactors are not.</li>
<li>India's NSG (Nuclear Suppliers Group) membership bid: India seeks entry despite not being NPT signatory — China is the main blocker. IAEA certification of India's safety record is India's key argument.</li>
<li>IAEA's role in Iran nuclear deal (JCPOA) monitoring — relevant to India's energy security (Iran-India oil imports, Chabahar Port).</li>
<li>India's nuclear power expansion: 22 reactors operational, 8 under construction — all civilian reactors under IAEA safeguards. Nuclear energy as part of India's clean energy transition.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">5.2 WTO (World Trade Organization)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Geneva | <b>Founded:</b> 1995 (successor to GATT 1947) | <b>Members:</b> 166 | <b>DG:</b> Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Nigeria, 2021–, first woman and first African to head WTO)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Global rules for international trade; provides forum for trade negotiations and dispute settlement (Dispute Settlement Body — DSB). Key agreements: GATT, GATS (services), TRIPS (IP), Agreement on Agriculture, TBT, SPS.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle — HIGH PRIORITY</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>India's key WTO positions:</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>Public stockholding for food security:</b> India insists on a permanent solution allowing it to subsidise food procurement beyond WTO limits for food security — the "peace clause" (Bali 2013) grants interim protection; India blocked Trade Facilitation Agreement ratification until resolved (2014).</li>
<li><b>TRIPS waiver for COVID-19 vaccines:</b> India (with South Africa) proposed waiving IP rights for vaccines/medicines during the pandemic — partial agreement reached MC12 (Geneva, 2022).</li>
<li><b>Fisheries subsidies agreement (MC12, 2022):</b> India had reservations about the timeline for developing countries — subsidy disciplines for illegal, unreported, unregulated (IUU) fishing.</li>
<li><b>India vs EU/US in WTO disputes:</b> e.g. solar panels (domestic content requirements under JNNSM — India lost to US), sugar export subsidies (Australia, Brazil, Guatemala challenged India's sugar scheme).</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Doha Development Round (launched 2001) — effectively stalled; India was a key player in its breakdown (2008 agricultural safeguard dispute).</li>
<li>WTO's Appellate Body crisis: US blocked appointments (2019–); body is non-functional — systemic challenge to global trade governance.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">5.3 ICC (International Criminal Court)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> The Hague | <b>Founded:</b> 2002 (Rome Statute) | <b>Members:</b> 124 states parties | <b>Prosecutor:</b> Karim Khan (UK, 2021–)</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Tries individuals (not states) for the most serious international crimes: genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and crime of aggression. Complementary to national courts — steps in when states are unable or unwilling to prosecute.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>India has NOT ratified the Rome Statute</b> — along with China, Russia, the US, and several others. India's concerns: potential misuse against sovereign decisions; jurisdiction issues; reluctance to allow foreign court to try Indian military/officials.</li>
<li>Distinguish ICC (criminal, individuals) from ICJ (civil, inter-state disputes) — a critical distinction for UPSC answers.</li>
<li>ICC issued arrest warrant for Russian President Putin (March 2023) over alleged deportation of Ukrainian children — raised legal and diplomatic questions; India's position: abstained from endorsing ICC's jurisdiction over the matter.</li>
<li>ICC vs South Africa: South Africa was obligated to arrest Putin at the BRICS Summit 2023 as an ICC member — instead Putin attended virtually. Shows tension between international law obligations and geopolitics.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">5.4 CTBTO (Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> Vienna | <b>Established:</b> 1996 Preparatory Commission | CTBT has been signed by 187 states, ratified by 178 — but <b>NOT entered into force</b> as 8 Annex 2 states (including India, Pakistan, China, US, Israel) have not ratified it.</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li><b>India has NOT signed the CTBT</b> — India's position: the CTBT is discriminatory as it does not commit nuclear states to disarmament (NPT Article VI) and does not include a time-bound framework for universal disarmament. India conducted Pokhran-II tests (1998) before CTBT could enter into force.</li>
<li>India maintains a unilateral moratorium on nuclear testing since 1998 (no more tests) — but refusing to sign CTBT retains strategic flexibility.</li>
<li>CTBT's International Monitoring System (IMS): global seismic/hydroacoustic/infrasound/radionuclide network; used to detect nuclear tests (e.g. North Korea's tests detected). India cooperates with IMS data sharing despite not ratifying.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="background-color:#3d6ea8; color:#ffffff; padding:8px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px;">5.5 OPCW (Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons)</h3>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>HQ:</b> The Hague | <b>Founded:</b> 1997 (Chemical Weapons Convention — CWC) | <b>Members:</b> 193 | <b>Nobel Peace Prize: 2013</b></div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:8px 12px; margin-bottom:6px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Mandate:</b> Implement the Chemical Weapons Convention; verifies destruction of chemical weapons stockpiles; investigates alleged use of chemical weapons (e.g. Syria).</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:24px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India is a member and destroyed its declared chemical weapons stockpile under OPCW verification — held up as a model of compliance.</li>
<li>OPCW's Syria investigation (Douma, 2018 attack) and Russia's obstruction — P5 veto politics in UNSC intersect here.</li>
<li>India's chemical industry and dual-use chemicals: Schedule 1 chemicals require OPCW reporting.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">6. UNSC Reform — A Critical Debate</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:12px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Why Reform is Needed</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>The UNSC was designed in 1945 for a post-WWII world. Its composition does not reflect the current multipolar world: Africa (~55 countries, 1.4 bn people) has no permanent seat; India (1.4 bn, world's 5th largest economy) is not permanent; Latin America and Southeast Asia are unrepresented among P5.</li>
<li>P5 veto has paralysed the Council on Syria, Ukraine, Gaza, and many other crises — eroding legitimacy.</li>
<li>Calls for reform have been ongoing since the 1990s; the UN Charter requires amendment via a 2/3 UNGA majority AND ratification by 2/3 of members including all P5 — China and Russia can block.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;">
<b>G4 Model (India, Japan, Germany, Brazil):</b> Seek 2 new permanent seats each for Asia, Africa, Europe (developed), Latin America + expansion of non-permanent seats to ~21 total. Backed by US, France, UK, Russia. <b>China opposes</b> (especially Japan and India's bids). G4 is the model most associated with India's campaign.
</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;">
<b>Uniting for Consensus (UfC / "Coffee Club"):</b> Led by Italy, Pakistan, South Korea, Argentina, Mexico — opposes creating new permanent seats; instead wants more non-permanent seats (elected, rotational). Pakistan's primary motivation is blocking India's permanent membership; Italy opposes Germany's bid.
</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;">
<b>C10 — African Group (Ezulwini Consensus, 2005):</b> Africa wants <b>2 permanent seats with veto</b> + 5 non-permanent seats. Insists Africa was denied a permanent seat unjustly in 1945. The veto demand is the main stumbling block — P5 are reluctant to expand the veto.
</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;">
<b>France-Mexico Veto Initiative (2013):</b> P5 members voluntarily restrain from using the veto in mass atrocity situations. 107 countries endorsed — but US, Russia, China rejected. Complemented by the Liechtenstein-led "Veto Initiative" (2022) — automatic UNGA debate on every P5 veto cast; passed by 83 votes.
</div>
<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Angle</b>
<ul style="margin:4px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>"UNSC reform is necessary for effective multilateralism" — classic UPSC Mains question; requires balancing India's self-interest with principled arguments.</li>
<li>India's arguments: demographic representation, economic weight, peacekeeping contributions, democratic values, civilisational heritage, Global South voice.</li>
<li>"Summit of the Future" (New York, Sept 2024): adopted a "Pact for the Future" — endorsed UNSC reform in principle, but specifics deferred; also launched a Global Digital Compact and Declaration on Future Generations.</li>
<li>China's ambiguity: China officially supports "increased African representation" but remains vague on expanding permanent members — strategic use of the issue to prevent US-aligned countries (India, Japan) from gaining permanent seats.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 style="background-color:#2a5298; color:#ffffff; padding:10px 16px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:20px;">7. India and the UN: Key Facts at a Glance</h2>

<div style="background-color:#eef3fb; border:1px solid #bed1f0; border-left:5px solid #2a5298; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:12px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>Membership &amp; Founding Role</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>India is a <b>founding member</b> of the UN (1945) — signed the UN Charter at San Francisco, even though India was still under British colonial rule (represented as British India).</li>
<li>India has been elected to the UNSC as a non-permanent member <b>8 times</b> — most recently 2021–22 (Asia-Pacific group), when India championed the theme "New Orientation for a Reformed Multilateralism."</li>
<li>India has consistently advocated for UN reform, expanded developing-country representation, and a rules-based multilateral order.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;"><b>UN Peacekeeping:</b> India is one of the <b>largest contributors of peacekeeping troops</b> historically — over 275,000 Indian troops deployed in 50+ missions since 1950 (Korea was first). Currently among the top 5 troop-contributing countries (TCCs). India has lost ~170+ peacekeepers — more than any other country. UN Peacekeeping Day: 29 May. India's contributions to missions in Congo, Lebanon (UNIFIL), South Sudan (UNMISS), etc.</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Financial Contribution:</b> India pays ~0.83% of the UN regular budget and ~0.17% of the peacekeeping budget — significantly below its proportional global weight; India pushes for reform of assessment scales so developing countries are not over-burdened.</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key UN Conventions India HAS Ratified:</b> UN Charter, UDHR (endorsed), CRC, CEDAW, CRPD, ICCPR, ICESCR, UNCLOS, Chemical Weapons Convention (OPCW), Biological Weapons Convention (BWC), NPT (as a non-nuclear weapons state — which India disputes as it is a de facto nuclear state), CBD, UNFCCC/Paris Agreement, UNCAC (anti-corruption).</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;"><b>Key Conventions India has NOT Ratified/Signed:</b> 1951 Refugee Convention, Rome Statute (ICC), CTBT, NPT (as a nuclear weapons state — India tests and maintains weapons), Convention on Cluster Munitions, Mine Ban Treaty (Ottawa Treaty on landmines).</div>
<div style="background-color:#dfe9f5; border-left:4px solid #2a5298; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:8px; border-radius:4px;"><b>India at UNGA:</b> Used UNGA as platform to launch: International Day of Yoga (2015), International Solar Alliance (ISA, with France), Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI), LiFE Mission, International Year of Millets (2023). India is active in the G77+China bloc (developing country coalition at the UN).</div>
<div style="background-color:#f5e9dc; border-left:4px solid #c9701b; padding:10px 14px; margin-bottom:20px; border-radius:4px;"><b>SDG Progress:</b> India's SDG score (Sustainable Development Report 2023): ranks ~112/166. Strong on SDG-7 (clean energy) and SDG-9 (infrastructure), lagging on SDG-2 (hunger), SDG-5 (gender), SDG-13 (climate action). India's VNRs submitted in 2017, 2020, 2023.</div>

<div style="background-color:#faf3e0; border:1px solid #ecd6b8; border-left:5px solid #c9701b; border-radius:6px; padding:12px 16px; margin-bottom:16px; width:100%; box-sizing:border-box;">
<b>UPSC Quick-Reference: "India is NOT a member/signatory of..."</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>NPT (as a nuclear-armed state)</li>
<li>CTBT (has not signed)</li>
<li>1951 Refugee Convention + 1967 Protocol</li>
<li>Rome Statute (ICC)</li>
<li>Convention on Cluster Munitions</li>
<li>Ottawa Treaty (Mine Ban)</li>
<li>Global Compact on Refugees (GCR)</li>
</ul>
<br>
<b>UPSC Quick-Reference: India IS a member/signatory of...</b>
<ul style="margin:6px 0 0 0; padding-left:20px;">
<li>UN (founding member), UNSC (non-permanent, 8 times)</li>
<li>All 8 ILO Fundamental Conventions (ratified last two in 2021)</li>
<li>IAEA safeguards agreements (civilian nuclear)</li>
<li>CWC (OPCW member, destroyed CW stockpile)</li>
<li>UNCLOS (but disputes its application to certain areas)</li>
<li>Paris Agreement / UNFCCC</li>
<li>CBD, Cartagena Protocol, Nagoya Protocol</li>
<li>UNCAC, UNTOC</li>
</ul>
</div>
